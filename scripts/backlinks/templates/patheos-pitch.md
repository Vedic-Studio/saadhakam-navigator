# Patheos — Hindu Channel Column Pitch

Target: **Patheos (Hindu channel)** — authority tier, priority #1, DR 80, dofollow.
Goal: a **recurring column**, not a one-off guest post (see model below). Recurring = an
author profile plus a sourced link on every post, which compounds far better than one link.

## How Patheos actually works (set expectations)
- Patheos hosts ~450 blogs across faith "channels" (Hindu, Buddhist, Catholic, etc.).
- Columnists are onboarded, then **self-publish** under light guidelines (no libel/profanity).
  The bar to *get* a column is having something worth reading and the discipline to post regularly.
- Pay is per-1000-views, small. Treat the **dofollow byline + audience** as the real value, not the pay.
- Diligence: Patheos has had public contributor churn and management friction (HuffPost, 2017).
  Fine to write for, but keep your canonical content on opensadhaka.com and link inward, never
  the reverse. Don't make Patheos your primary home for anything important.

## Before you send (2 minutes)
1. Find a name. Check the [Hindu channel](https://www.patheos.com/hindu) and recent posts for the
   current managing editor / channel editor. The generic contact form is the fallback, a named
   editor is far better. Replace `[Editor name]` below.
2. Have one writing sample link ready. Default: `https://opensadhaka.com/what-is-sanatan-dharma`
   (shows you can write the tradition authoritatively). Alt: a comparative piece such as
   `nietzsche-and-vedanta` or `consciousness-hard-problem-vedanta` to show range.
3. Send from a real opensadhaka address with your name. Fill `[email]`.

## The pitch (ready to send)

**Subject:** Column pitch: scoping the viral "ancient India" claims with primary sources

Hi [Editor name],

I run opensadhaka.com, an English-language reference for Sanatan Dharma: Vedanta, the Shaiva,
Shakta and Vaishnava schools, the sacred texts, and practice. I read the Hindu channel regularly,
and one thing I keep wishing existed there is an honest, sourced treatment of the viral "ancient
India discovered X" claims.

You know the ones. Sushruta invented plastic surgery. The Surya Siddhanta is accurate to within
1% of NASA. India invented zero. They circulate constantly, usually in one of two flavors:
uncritical celebration or flat dismissal. Both skip the actual scholarship, which is often more
interesting than either.

I'd like to write a regular column for the Hindu channel that takes one claim at a time and scopes
it properly: what the primary source actually says, what holds up, what gets exaggerated, and what
is genuinely remarkable once you set the hype aside. A clear verdict per sub-claim, every source cited.

This isn't speculative. We maintain a claim library that already does this for fifteen claims, each
broken into scoped sub-claims with verdicts and primary-source citations. Three I'd open with:

1. Did an ancient Indian surgeon invent plastic surgery? What the Sushruta Samhita actually says.
2. Is the Surya Siddhanta really "within 1% of NASA"? A line-by-line check.
3. Did women compose the Vedas? The case for the women rishis of the Rigveda.

Writing sample, for voice and sourcing standard: https://opensadhaka.com/what-is-sanatan-dharma

I can sustain a piece every two weeks, and I'd cross-promote each one to our readers and newsletter.
Are you taking new Hindu-channel columnists right now? If a full sample post would help, I'll write
one on whichever of the three you find most interesting.

Thanks for the work you do on the channel.

Ankit Mishra
Founder, opensadhaka.com

## Link strategy (keep it editorial, never spammy)
- The dofollow win is the **author byline** linking to opensadhaka.com. That alone is the goal.
- In-text, link only where it genuinely helps the reader: "the full sourced breakdown, with the
  Sanskrit and the secondary literature, is on opensadhaka.com". One or two per post, max.
- Never keyword-stuff anchors. Patheos editors and Google both penalize it, and it cheapens the brand.

## Companion move (do this in parallel — high leverage)
The in-text links land best if the claim pages are **live and public**. They aren't yet (the KB
claim files are internal). Publishing the claim library as public pages:
  - makes every Patheos in-text citation resolve to a real sourced page,
  - is itself a premium SEO/GEO asset (scoped + primary-sourced = exactly what AI engines cite),
  - gives every future authority-tier pitch concrete artifacts to point at.
Minimum viable version: publish the 3 claims behind the opening columns first.
