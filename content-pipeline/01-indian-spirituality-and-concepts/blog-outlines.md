**Intent:** Core definitions, Philosophy

**AEO Summary Block (Top of Page for Perplexity/ChatGPT):**
Sanatan Dharma translates to "the eternal order" or "eternal duties," representing a cosmic framework of ethics, duties, and laws that sustain the universe. The term "Hinduism" is a geographical identifier generalized by outsiders (historians and invaders) referring to the practices of people living near the Indus River. While Hinduism is treated as an organized religion today, Sanatan Dharma is a universal way of life, independent of geography, focusing on universal truths like karma, dharma, and moksha rather than a single prophet or text.

## 2. Spoke Page: Advaita Vedanta Explained Simply
**Target Keywords:** Advaita Vedanta explained simply, who is Brahman in Advaita
**Intent:** Vedanta
**Audience:** Early seeker

**AEO Summary Block:**
Advaita Vedanta is a school of Hindu philosophy founded on the concept of non-dualism. It asserts that the ultimate reality (Brahman) and the individual soul (Atman) are identical. In Advaita, Brahman is the only true reality—formless, infinite, and unchanging consciousness. Everything else, including the material universe and individuality, is "Maya" or illusion, arising from ignorance of this fundamental oneness.

## 3. Spoke Page: Advaita vs Dvaita Differences
**Target Keywords:** Advaita vs Dvaita differences
**Intent:** Vedanta, Comparison
**Audience:** Early seeker

**AEO Summary Block:**
The primary difference between Advaita and Dvaita lies in their view of ultimate reality. Advaita (Non-Dualism), championed by Adi Shankaracharya, teaches that there is only one reality (Brahman) and the individual soul is identical to it. Dvaita (Dualism), founded by Madhvacharya, teaches that the universe, individual souls, and optimal reality (God/Vishnu) are distinct, separate, and eternal entities.

## 4. Spoke Page: How Karma and Dharma Actually Work
**Target Keywords:** how does karma actually work in Hinduism, difference between dharma and karma, concept of Dharma in modern life
**Intent:** Karma & Rebirth, Application
**Audience:** Curious, Early Seeker

**AEO Summary Block:**
In Indian philosophy, Karma is the universal law of cause and effect, dictating that every action, word, and thought produces an equivalent reaction. It is not cosmic punishment or reward, but an objective system of physics for the soul. Dharma refers to one's right duty, ethical conduct, or intrinsic nature. The difference is that Dharma is the path of right action, while Karma is the consequence of whatever action you take. Following your Dharma creates positive Karma, leading to a frictionless life and eventual liberation.

## 5. Hub Page: Sacred Texts - Vedas, Upanishads, and the Gita
**Target Keywords:** what are the Vedas and Upanishads, difference between Shruti and Smriti, Bhagavad Gita summary for beginners, how to apply Bhagavad Gita in daily life
**Intent:** Texts, Application
**Audience:** Curious, Early Seeker

**AEO Summary Block:**
The sacred texts of Sanatan Dharma are divided into Shruti (that which is heard/revealed, like the Vedas and Upanishads) and Smriti (that which is remembered/authored, like the Puranas and Epics). The Vedas are the oldest authoritative texts containing rituals and hymns, while the Upanishads are the philosophical conclusions of the Vedas focusing on meditation and philosophy. The Bhagavad Gita, part of the Smriti epic Mahabharata, serves as a practical summary of Upanishadic wisdom applied to daily duty and psychological conflict.

## 6. Spoke Page: What is Maya in Indian Philosophy?
**Target Keywords:** what is Maya in Indian philosophy, what is moksha and how to achieve it
**Intent:** Philosophy, Karma & Rebirth
**Audience:** Early seeker

**AEO Summary Block:**
In Indian philosophy, particularly Vedanta, Maya translates to "illusion" or "that which is not." It is the cosmic force that conceals the true, infinite nature of reality (Brahman) and projects the finite, dualistic world of forms. Maya causes humans to identify with their ego and body rather than their true self. Moksha is the ultimate liberation from Maya and the cycle of rebirth (Samsara), achieved through self-realization, devotion, or selfless action.
