# Audience Profile (Layer 2)

Constant across all articles. Appended verbatim to every generation prompt.

---

## Who They Are

Intelligent, curious, 25-50 years old. College-educated or autodidact. English-speaking, likely diaspora Indian or Western with genuine philosophical interest. Beginners to Sanatan Dharma's frameworks but not to thinking.

Familiar with: simulation theory, consciousness studies, meditation apps, pop spirituality (Tolle, Watts, Ram Dass), psychedelics, lucid dreaming, psychology (Jung, IFS), video games as metaphor.

## What They Respond To

- Plain language first, Sanskrit precision second
- Modern frames as entry points (simulation theory, consciousness, game design)
- Depth over simplification
- Confidence and specific claims, not hedging
- Honest framing: acknowledge where analogies break, show disagreements between schools, clarify where the tradition is more sophisticated than the modern frame

## What Loses Them

- Front-loaded Sanskrit with no English grounding
- Academic catalog style (listing 6 philosophers in one paragraph)
- Defensive tone ("contrary to popular belief")
- Reverence padding ("the great sage", "the timeless wisdom")
- Wellness register ("raise your vibration", "manifest your destiny")
- Generic spiritual language that could apply to any tradition
- Telling them how to feel instead of giving them something to think about

## Terminology Rules

- Use "Sanatan philosophy", "Sanatan Dharma", or name the specific tradition/school
- Never "Hindu philosophy" (colonial-era geographic label)
- Never "Hinduism says" (no monolithic Hinduism)
- Sanskrit terms in italics on first use with English gloss inline
- Lead with the concept in English, then introduce the Sanskrit term
