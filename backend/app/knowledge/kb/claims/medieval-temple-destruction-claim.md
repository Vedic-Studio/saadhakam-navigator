## Plain-language statement

Hindu temples were destroyed, desecrated, and in many cases converted into mosques during the period of Muslim political rule in India (roughly the Ghaznavid raids onwards through the Sultanate and Mughal eras). The contested questions are: **how many** such destructions are documented to the standard of named-temple, named-perpetrator, dated-event; **what motivated** them (religious iconoclasm, political signaling against defeated rival rulers, plunder, or general warfare); and **what conclusion** one can responsibly draw about the broader civilizational story from this record. Sita Ram Goel's two-volume *Hindu Temples: What Happened to Them* is the most cited maximalist catalog (≈ 2000+ sites in the Vol I appendix, drawn largely from ASI reports and the Islamic-source list of Maulana Abdul Hai). Richard Eaton's *Temple Desecration and Indo-Muslim States* (2000) is the most cited minimalist counterpoint (≈ 80 well-attested cases on his strict criteria). The truth almost certainly lives between these two enumerations — not at either pole.

## Decomposed sub-claims

1. **"Islamic chronicles themselves describe the destruction of Hindu temples."** — *Strongly supported.* This is the cleanest part of the record. Primary Persian and Arabic chronicles (*Tarikh-i-Yamini* on Mahmud of Ghazni at Somnath, *Chach Nama*, *Tabaqat-i-Nasiri*, *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi*, *Babur Nama*, *Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri*, *Maasir-i-Alamgiri* on Aurangzeb at Kashi Vishwanath and Keshava Deva Mathura) explicitly narrate the destruction or conversion of named temples. Volume II of Goel is essentially a curated translation of these passages. (To be filled: specific passages with edition citations.)

2. **"The Archaeological Survey of India has documented mosques built on the foundations of, or using the spolia of, demolished temples."** — *Strongly supported in named cases; contested in aggregate count.* Cases with strong archaeological consensus include the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque at Delhi (built with columns from Jain and Hindu temples; site inscription says as much), the Adina mosque at Pandua, the Atala mosque at Jaunpur, and several others. The Ayodhya, Kashi Vishwanath, and Mathura Keshava Deva cases are the most legally and politically charged; archaeological findings at Ayodhya (ASI 2003 report) have been read different ways by different historians. (To be filled: site-by-site verdict table.)

3. **"Around 2000 specific temples were destroyed and replaced by mosques during the Islamic period."** — *Contested.* This is the headline number from Goel Vol I's appendix. The appendix is largely a compilation from ASI reports and the Urdu work *Hindustan Islami Ahad Mein* by Maulana Abdul Hai (an Islamic-source listing of mosques built on temple sites). The list is real and the underlying sources exist, but the criteria for what counts as a "destroyed temple" vary across entries — some have strong evidence, some are repeat-counts of the same site under different names, some rest on local oral tradition recorded by 19th-century gazetteer compilers. Eaton's counter-enumeration applies stricter criteria (named temple + named perpetrator + dated event + corroborating source) and arrives at ≈ 80 cases for the period 1192–1729. (To be filled: source-criticism of the Goel appendix and the Eaton list, side by side.)

4. **"The destructions were primarily driven by religious iconoclasm in the doctrinal sense."** — *Partially supported, contested in degree.* Eaton's central argument is that most well-attested destructions follow a political-signaling pattern: the **royal temple** of a defeated rival ruler was singled out, because it housed the state deity that legitimized that ruler — destruction was an act of conquest, not generalized religious cleansing. Eaton notes that temples in territory already under Islamic rule, and temples not associated with a defeated dynasty, were generally left alone (and in some periods actively patronized — e.g., Aurangzeb's own grants to certain temples, documented by Truschke and others). Goel and the Hindu nationalist reading treat the religious-doctrinal motivation as primary; Eaton and the academic mainstream treat it as one factor in a political-religious complex. (To be filled: side-by-side framing with the specific Eaton passages and the specific Goel rebuttal.)

5. **"Aurangzeb personally ordered the destruction of thousands of temples."** — *Contested; partially supported.* Aurangzeb did issue farmans ordering the destruction of specific temples (Kashi Vishwanath 1669, Keshava Deva Mathura 1670 are the best-documented), and his reign saw a clear policy shift compared to Akbar's. The aggregate number ("thousands") rests on a single often-cited passage in *Maasir-i-Alamgiri* whose translation and scope have been disputed (Truschke 2017 is the standard revisionist re-reading; J.F. Richards and others occupy a middle position). Aurangzeb also issued farmans **protecting** specific temples (Brahmins of Banaras, Jangamabari math, etc.) — these documents are extant. The full picture is more complicated than either "Aurangzeb the iconoclast" or "Aurangzeb the misunderstood pluralist." (To be filled: the specific *Maasir-i-Alamgiri* passage with multiple translations, and the protective farmans.)

6. **"The Somnath temple was destroyed by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1026 CE in a single iconic event."** — *Partially supported; the popular framing collapses a longer history.* Mahmud's 1026 raid is historically attested in Persian sources. But Romila Thapar's *Somanatha: The Many Voices of a History* (2004) demonstrates that the modern memory of "Somnath" has been constructed from multiple sources (Persian chronicles, Sanskrit inscriptions, Jain accounts, colonial-era reconstructions) that don't all agree, and that the temple was rebuilt, destroyed again, and rebuilt several times across centuries. The 1026 event is real; the singular-trauma narrative built around it is partly a 19th–20th century construction. (To be filled: Thapar's specific source-critical moves, and the Goel/right-wing counter-reading.)

7. **"The Ayodhya, Mathura, and Kashi sites have demonstrable Hindu temple substrates beneath their later mosques."** — *Each case is separate; the verdicts differ.* The Babri Masjid / Ram Janmabhoomi case has the most archaeological investigation (ASI 2003 report, debated by KK Muhammed, Suraj Bhan, and others; the Supreme Court 2019 judgment cited the report). The Kashi Vishwanath / Gyanvapi case has Aurangzeb's own farman and visible architectural spolia. The Mathura Keshava Deva / Shahi Eidgah case is well-documented in *Maasir-i-Alamgiri*. Each of these three deserves its own sub-claim file with case-specific evidence. (To be filled: split into three site-specific claim files when capacity exists.)

8. **"Hindu sources are largely silent about the destruction of temples."** — *Contested.* The Hindu nationalist reading often asserts this silence as evidence of either suppression or trauma. Academic historians (e.g., Cynthia Talbot on the Kakatiya-era inscriptional record, Phillip Wagoner on Vijayanagara) have shown that Hindu and Telugu-Sanskrit sources of the period do reference Muslim military encounters, sometimes obliquely and sometimes directly. The "silence" claim is partly an artifact of which Hindu sources scholars looked at. (To be filled.)

## Scope boundaries

- This claim is **not** denying that temple destruction occurred. It did, repeatedly, across centuries, and the primary Islamic sources themselves describe it.
- This claim is **not** endorsing the "every mosque in India sits on a destroyed temple" maximalism. Most mosques in India were built on land that was not a Hindu temple. The contested-site cases are a specific subset.
- This claim is **not** a theory of Islam-as-such. Both maximalist and minimalist readings agree that periods, rulers, and policies varied enormously — Akbar's policy was not Aurangzeb's, and neither was Ghazni's.
- This claim **is** the place where the Goel maximalist enumeration and the Eaton minimalist enumeration are held together so that articles can cite either pole with the right scope, rather than collapsing into one or the other.
- This claim is the **hub**; the specific contested sites (Ayodhya, Kashi, Mathura, Somnath, Qutub complex) deserve their own site-specific claim files in a future pass.
