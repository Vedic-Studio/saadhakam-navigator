## Definition

In Vaisheshika, **anu** / **paramanu** are the smallest, indivisible units out of which composite physical bodies are built. The theory specifies that these atoms are *partless*, *eternal*, and belong to four elemental types — earth, water, fire, air (akasha, space, is treated differently as an all-pervading medium). Atoms combine first into **dvyanukas** (dyads of two atoms), then into **tryanukas** (triads of dyads), and larger composites from there.

## Where it appears

- [Vaisheshika Sutras](../texts/vaisheshika-sutras.md) — the foundational articulation
- **Prashastapada's Padartha-dharma-sangraha** (~6th c. CE) — the systematic early exposition that later tradition engages with
- Later Nyaya-Vaisheshika commentarial tradition

## Interpretive history

- **Early sutra tradition**: atoms are reached by inference from perceived composites; they are imperceptible but inferentially necessary
- **Prashastapada**: systematizes the combination rules (dyads, triads)
- **Later Nyaya-Vaisheshika**: defends the atomist framework against Buddhist and Jaina atomisms, against Advaita's monism, and against various anti-atomist critiques
- **Modern philosophical treatments**: emphasize that Vaisheshika atomism is *metaphysics and natural philosophy*, not experimental physics, and is reached through inference rather than measurement

## Modern bridges

- **Inference to the best explanation**: Vaisheshika reaches atoms by inference from observed composites — "whatever has parts can be divided; if division does not terminate, composites are impossible; therefore ultimate indivisible parts must exist." This is structurally parallel to modern scientific realism's inferential arguments for unobservable entities. *Breaks because* the classical argument is a philosophical derivation, not a deduction from experiment.
- **Atomic theory**: superficially, atoms-combining-into-composites looks like the modern atomic picture. *Breaks because* Vaisheshika atoms are partless, eternal, and inferred philosophically, while modern atoms are experimentally characterized, have internal structure (protons, neutrons, electrons), and can be split.
- **Category ontology**: the atomism sits inside a broader framework of six padarthas (categories), which is itself a philosophical move comparable to modern metaphysics' attempts at category ontology.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"Kanada discovered atoms before Democritus."** Dating is too uncertain for clean priority claims, and both Democritean and Vaisheshika atomisms are *philosophical*, not experimental.
- **"Vaisheshika atoms are the same as modern atoms."** Structurally similar (indivisible units that combine), metaphysically very different (partless and eternal vs experimentally characterized and composite).
- **"India had atomic theory and therefore had modern physics."** The framework is an important early contribution to the *philosophical* history of atomism, not a precursor to experimental atomic physics.
- **"All Indian atomisms are the same."** Buddhist, Jaina, and Vaisheshika atomisms differ meaningfully. The diversity is part of what makes India's contribution interesting.
