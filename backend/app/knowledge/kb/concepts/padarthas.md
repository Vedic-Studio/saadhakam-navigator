## What it is

**Padartha** (literally "the meaning of a word" or "that which corresponds to a term") is the technical Vaisheshika term for an **ontological category** — a fundamental kind of thing that exists. The Vaisheshika darshana is distinctive among classical Indian philosophical schools for developing an explicit, systematic ontology: it catalogs the basic categories of existence and analyzes how everything in the world falls under one of them. The framework is sometimes compared to Aristotle's categories but is developed independently and differs in significant respects.

Kanada's Vaisheshika Sutras enumerate **six padarthas** (with a seventh added later). The classical six are:

1. **Dravya** (substance) — the bearer of qualities and actions; includes the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, ether), time, space, self (atman), and mind (manas) — nine substances total
2. **Guna** (quality) — properties that inhere in substances; includes 24 qualities in the mature system (color, taste, smell, touch, number, dimension, separateness, conjunction, disjunction, priority, posteriority, cognition, pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, effort, weight, fluidity, viscosity, sound, etc.)
3. **Karma** (action) — the activity of substances (motion, contraction, expansion, etc.); distinct from quality
4. **Samanya** (universal / generality) — the shared character that makes particulars instances of a kind (e.g., cowness that makes cows cows)
5. **Vishesha** (ultimate particularity) — the feature that distinguishes one eternal atom from another; this is a distinctive Vaisheshika notion — **atoms need distinguishers because they are otherwise qualitatively identical**
6. **Samavaya** (inherence) — the relation by which qualities, actions, universals, and particularities are "in" their substances; inherence is itself a distinct category because it is not a substance, quality, or action

A seventh category, **abhava** (non-existence/absence), was added by later Nyaya-Vaisheshika (and is already implicit in the tradition), giving the standard medieval list of **seven padarthas**. Abhava has four sub-kinds: prior non-existence, posterior non-existence, mutual non-existence, and absolute non-existence.

## Why the framework matters

- **Explicit ontology**: Vaisheshika is the classical Indian philosophical school that most explicitly frames its project as "here is a complete catalogue of what exists." This makes it an exceptional comparative interlocutor for European ontology from Aristotle through medieval scholasticism and into contemporary analytic metaphysics.
- **Distinguishing relations from relata**: the inclusion of **samavaya (inherence) as its own category** is a striking move — it treats the relation by which qualities inhere in substances as itself an irreducible constituent of reality, not just as a derived relational fact.
- **Distinguishing ultimate particulars**: **vishesha** addresses a problem that arises when atoms are qualitatively identical — if two atoms share all their properties, what distinguishes them? Vaisheshika answers: an ultimate particularity, itself a real category of existence.
- **The negation category**: **abhava** gives Vaisheshika a sophisticated treatment of negative facts (e.g., "there is no pot on the table") that handles what modern philosophers call "the problem of negative facts" in a distinctive way.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"Padarthas are the same as Aristotle's categories."** There are overlaps (substance, quality, action) but also significant differences. Aristotle has ten categories; Vaisheshika has six (or seven). The treatment of relations and particulars is different. Comparison is productive; equation is misleading.
- **"Vaisheshika atoms are the same as modern atoms."** Vaisheshika anu/paramanu are eternal, indivisible, qualitatively distinguished point-substances — they are a metaphysical posit, not a physical hypothesis. See [Vaisheshika anu/paramanu](vaisheshika-anu.md).
- **"The seven padarthas are fixed canonically."** The seventh (abhava) was added to the tradition later; there was internal debate about whether non-existence should be a category. Treat the list as a mature Nyaya-Vaisheshika settlement, not an eternal canon.

## Modern bridges

- **Analytic metaphysics**: Vaisheshika's ontology is recognizably a realist, category-based ontology with substances, properties, relations, and (controversially) negative facts. It engages productively with contemporary debates in analytic metaphysics about universals, tropes, and relations. *Breaks because* Vaisheshika's home problems (liberation, apoha, the metaphysics of Vedic authority) are not those of contemporary analytic metaphysics.
- **Mereology**: Vaisheshika's treatment of how atoms combine into dyads, triads, and larger composites is a theory of parthood that can be compared with classical and contemporary mereology.
