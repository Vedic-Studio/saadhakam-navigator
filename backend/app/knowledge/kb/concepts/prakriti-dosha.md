## Definition

In classical Ayurveda:

- **Dosha** (tridosha) — the three biological humors **vata**, **pitta**, and **kapha**, whose balance and imbalance provide the explanatory framework for health and disease
- **Prakriti** — a person's constitutional type, determined by the relative proportions of the three doshas established at conception. Classical Ayurveda recognizes seven primary prakriti categories (three mono-doshic, three dual-doshic, one tri-doshic balanced).

The framework is both diagnostic (for understanding individual vulnerability to disease) and therapeutic (for guiding diet, lifestyle, and treatment).

## Interpretive history

- **Classical tradition**: dosha and prakriti are explanatory primitives, used throughout the diagnostic and therapeutic apparatus
- **Colonial-era reception**: often dismissed as "unscientific humoral medicine"
- **Late 20th–21st century**: growing interest in *investigating* (not endorsing) whether prakriti classification correlates with measurable biological variation
- **Current state**: the research is a legitimate, cautious program. Studies (including a Nature Scientific Reports genome-wide association study) have looked for genetic correlates of prakriti categories. Results are presented as *preliminary* and as motivation for further research, not as validation of the full classical doctrine.

## Modern bridges

- **Phenotypic classification for personalized medicine**: the prakriti framework is a phenotypic-classification approach that *predates and parallels* modern personalized-medicine ambitions. It asks the same general question: can we tailor intervention to the individual's constitution? *Breaks because* modern personalized medicine aims for molecular and genetic specificity that prakriti does not claim, and the classical classification is based on observable clinical signs rather than molecular assays.
- **Whole-systems chronic-disease management**: the framework's attention to diet, sleep, daily rhythm, and mental state resonates with modern chronic-disease guidelines. *Breaks because* the causal models differ: classical Ayurveda posits dosha imbalance, modern medicine posits biochemical pathways.
- **Temperament typologies**: prakriti's three-fold classification loosely parallels various modern temperament and personality typologies. *Breaks because* prakriti is primarily a *physiological* classification, not a psychological one, even though it has psychological implications.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"Dosha = modern biomarker."** Dosha is a classical explanatory framework for constitution and pathology, not a direct equivalent of a measurable biomarker. Studies that look for biological correlates should be cited as *investigating* the framework, not as having *validated* it wholesale.
- **"The Nature paper proved that prakriti is genetically determined."** The Nature Scientific Reports study is preliminary and explicitly presents itself as a starting point for further research. It is a legitimate scientific engagement with a classical framework, not a confirmation of all classical Ayurvedic doctrine.
- **"Ayurveda has always been evidence-based."** Classical Ayurveda has clinical reasoning and accumulated observation, but "evidence-based" in the modern biostatistical sense is a 20th-century concept. The WHO framing of "evidence-based integration of traditional medicine" is the responsible bridge.
- **"All Ayurvedic remedies are safe because they are natural."** Modern integration work explicitly addresses quality, contamination, dosing, and drug-interaction concerns.
