## What it is

The **Upavedas** ("secondary Vedas" or "applied Vedas") are a classical classification of four practical, applied knowledge systems that the tradition attached to the four Vedas. Unlike the Vedangas, which are *auxiliary sciences for preserving the Veda*, the Upavedas are *applied sciences derived from the Veda*. The standard list is:

1. **Ayurveda** — medicine (attached to Atharvaveda, sometimes to Rigveda)
2. **Dhanurveda** — military science and archery (attached to Yajurveda)
3. **Gandharvaveda** — music and performing arts (attached to Samaveda)
4. **Sthapatyaveda / Vastuvidya** — architecture, engineering, and town-planning (attached to Atharvaveda, sometimes treated as separate)

The list is traditional and not perfectly fixed. Different classical sources give slightly different attributions (e.g., whether architecture is an Upaveda or a separate discipline), and the category is more a retrospective organizing frame than a formally canonized corpus of texts.

## Where each sits in the broader tradition

- [**Ayurveda**](../shastras/ayurveda-medicine.md) — the medical tradition running from Atharvaveda healing hymns through the classical compendia (Charaka, Sushruta, Vagbhata). This is the most textually developed and institutionally continuous Upaveda. See also [Prakriti & Dosha](prakriti-dosha.md) and the [Ayurgenomics claim](../claims/ayurgenomics-prakriti-claim.md).
- [**Dhanurveda**](../shastras/dhanurveda-martial.md) — the military-science tradition, covering weapons, training, battle formations (vyuhas), and martial ethics. See also [Dharmayuddha vs Kutayuddha](dharmayuddha-vs-kutayuddha.md).
- [**Natyashastra / Gandharvaveda**](../shastras/natyashastra-gandharvaveda.md) — the tradition of music, dance, and performing arts. Its foundational text is **Bharata's Natya Shastra** (dated roughly 200 BCE to 200 CE), a comprehensive treatise on dramaturgy covering acting, music, dance, stagecraft, and aesthetic theory (rasa). Modern Indian classical music and dance descend from this tradition. See also [Natya Shastra (text)](../texts/natya-shastra.md) and [Bharata Muni](../people/bharata-muni.md).
- [**Silpashastra / Sthapatyaveda**](../shastras/silpashastra-sthapatyaveda.md) — the tradition of architecture, temple-building, iconography, and town-planning. Foundational texts include the **Mayamatam**, the **Manasara**, and the **Vishvakarma-vastushastra**. The tradition combines practical engineering, aesthetic theory, and ritual geometry.

## Why the framework matters

- **It recognizes applied knowledge as Vedic-derivative rather than secular.** The classification treats medicine, warfare, music, and architecture as knowledge systems that descend from the Veda and are authorized by that lineage. Whether or not one accepts the lineage claim historically, this is the self-understanding of the tradition.
- **It gives article writers a clean map of "applied Indian knowledge systems."** Most popular discussions of "ancient Indian science" concern Ayurveda, surgery, or mathematics. The Upaveda framework is the tradition's own taxonomy of applied knowledge, and it includes domains (music, architecture) that the modern "Indian science" conversation often forgets.
- **It clarifies what each tradition is attached to.** Ayurveda's attachment to Atharvaveda reflects the historical origin of Indian medicine in Atharvavedic healing hymns (see [Ayurveda shastra](../shastras/ayurveda-medicine.md) Stage 1 → Stage 2). Dhanurveda's attachment to Yajurveda reflects the ritual-warfare link in the later Vedic period.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"The Upavedas are texts."** No. Each Upaveda is a *tradition* and a *classification*, not a single text. Ayurveda's textual core is Charaka + Sushruta + Vagbhata (not a single "Ayurveda-Veda" book). Natya Shastra is the canonical Gandharvaveda text but not the entirety of the tradition.
- **"The list is fixed and canonical."** The list is traditional and varies in different sources. Some classical authorities include Arthashastra as an Upaveda; others treat it separately. Some include Sthapatyaveda; others fold it under Atharvaveda.
- **"Upavedas are 'lower' than Vedangas."** The prefix *upa-* means "secondary/adjacent," not "lesser." The distinction is functional: Vedangas preserve the Veda; Upavedas apply Vedic knowledge to practical domains. Both are Smriti-level.
