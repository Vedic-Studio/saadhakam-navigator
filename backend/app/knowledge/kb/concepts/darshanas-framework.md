## What it is

The classical Indian philosophical tradition recognizes **six orthodox (astika) darshanas** — "ways of seeing" or philosophical systems that accept Vedic authority and have developed mature, debated, commentarial traditions. They are:

1. **Nyaya** — logic and epistemology
2. **Vaisheshika** — ontology, categories, and atomism
3. **Samkhya** — dualist cosmology of purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (matter)
4. **Yoga** — psychology and practice of liberation, building on Samkhya metaphysics
5. **Purva Mimamsa** — ritual hermeneutics and the philosophy of interpretation
6. **Uttara Mimamsa / Vedanta** — philosophy of the Upanishads and the nature of Brahman

These are traditionally grouped in three pairs by topical affinity: **Nyaya–Vaisheshika** (logic/ontology), **Samkhya–Yoga** (dualism/practice), and **Mimamsa–Vedanta** (text interpretation, ritual vs. philosophical). Each darshana has a foundational sutra text, a commentarial lineage, and live internal debates.

## The three pairs

### Nyaya–Vaisheshika (logic and ontology)

- **Nyaya** developed systematic epistemology and logic, articulating pramanas (means of knowledge), the anumana (inference) syllogism, and the structure of debate. See [Pramana (means of knowledge)](pramana-epistemology.md).
- **Vaisheshika** developed systematic ontology, cataloguing the **padarthas** (categories of existence) and proposing atomism. See [Vaisheshika anu / paramanu](vaisheshika-anu.md).
- The two schools were historically fused into a single **Nyaya-Vaisheshika** tradition whose standard exposition is Prashastapada's Padartha-dharma-sangraha.

### Samkhya–Yoga (dualism and practice)

- **Samkhya** is the oldest systematic Indian philosophy of mind-and-matter dualism. It posits **purusha** (consciousness, inactive) and **prakriti** (matter, active, evolves into the phenomenal world through the interplay of three **gunas** — sattva, rajas, tamas).
- **Yoga** inherits Samkhya's metaphysics and adds a disciplined practice (the eightfold ashtanga) for isolating purusha from prakriti, culminating in kaivalya (isolation/liberation).
- Yoga accepts a personal Ishvara; Samkhya is formally atheistic (though some strands admit Ishvara).

### Mimamsa–Vedanta (hermeneutics and metaphysics of text)

- **Purva Mimamsa** (earlier inquiry) focuses on the interpretation of the ritual portions of the Vedas — what the ritual commands mean, how to read them, how apparent contradictions are resolved. Its hermeneutic methods became the template for much of classical Indian textual interpretation.
- **Uttara Mimamsa** (later inquiry) = **Vedanta**, focuses on the philosophical portions (Upanishads) and the nature of Brahman. Vedanta later splits into sub-schools: Advaita (Shankara), Vishishtadvaita (Ramanuja), Dvaita (Madhva), and others. See `backend/app/knowledge/domains/advaita-vedanta.md` for the Advaita treatment.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"The darshanas are six versions of the same thing."** No — they are rival systems with different fundamental commitments. Samkhya dualism, Vaisheshika realist pluralism, and Advaita Vedanta non-dualism are categorically different metaphysics.
- **"Astika schools all believe in God."** No. "Astika" technically means "accepting Vedic authority," not "believing in a creator God." Mimamsa is formally silent on God; Samkhya is formally atheist; Yoga accepts Ishvara but as a meditational focus, not a creator.
- **"The six darshanas are the whole of Indian philosophy."** No. Buddhist and Jain schools (**nastika** / non-Vedic) are also major philosophical traditions with rigorous logic, metaphysics, and debate, and they are in direct conversation with the darshanas throughout classical Indian history.
- **"Each darshana has a single canonical position."** Each has internal debates and multiple sub-schools. Advaita vs. Vishishtadvaita vs. Dvaita Vedanta are all "Vedanta" but disagree sharply on Brahman, soul, and world.

## Modern bridges

- **Philosophy of mind**: Samkhya's purusha-prakriti split is a clear two-level model of consciousness vs. its objects, structurally parallel to some modern philosophy-of-mind positions. *Breaks because* Samkhya is a metaphysics of liberation, not an explanatory theory of neural correlates.
- **Epistemology**: Nyaya's typology of pramanas (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) is a systematic epistemology that early modern Western epistemology would recognize as a peer tradition. *Breaks because* Nyaya has its own concerns (the structure of inference, the role of testimony) that do not map cleanly onto Humean or Kantian categories.
- **Philosophy of language and hermeneutics**: Mimamsa's theory of meaning and textual interpretation is an extraordinarily rigorous hermeneutic tradition, with sophisticated positions on what a sentence means, how commands differ from descriptions, and how to handle apparent contradictions. *Breaks because* Mimamsa's target text is the Veda and its concerns are ritual, not literary.
