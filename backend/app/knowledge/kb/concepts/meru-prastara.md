## Definition

The Meru-prastara is a triangular arrangement of binomial coefficients discussed in the Indian prosody tradition for counting how many metrical patterns have a given number of syllables of each type (short/long, laghu/guru). It is structurally equivalent to the triangular array now widely known in European mathematics as Pascal's triangle.

## Where it appears

- Discussed in Halayudha's Mrita-Sanjivani (~10th c. CE) as a commentary on Pingala's [Chandas Sutra](../texts/chandas-sutra.md)
- The prosodic tradition that precedes Halayudha is where the problem originates — enumerating metrical patterns — even if the explicit triangular arrangement is clearest in Halayudha

## Interpretive history

- **Early prosodic context**: the combinatorial problem (how many meters of length *n* have *k* laghu syllables?) is what generates the triangle
- **Halayudha makes it explicit**: the triangular arrangement and its properties are clearly articulated in his commentary
- **Modern history of mathematics**: treats the Meru-prastara as an important early articulation of binomial-coefficient reasoning and links it to subsequent mathematical traditions in India

## Modern bridges

- **Pascal's triangle**: structurally equivalent. The Meru-prastara arranges the same numbers in a triangular form and uses them for the same kind of counting problem. *Breaks because* structural equivalence is not historical identity — Pascal's triangle has its own separate history in Europe, and conflating the two erases two distinct intellectual traditions.
- **Combinatorics pedagogy**: the recurrence used to build the triangle ("each entry is the sum of the two above it") is a standard modern pedagogical approach. *Breaks because* the classical tradition did not frame it as modern combinatorics does.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"The Meru-prastara IS Pascal's triangle."** Structurally equivalent, not historically identical. Writing that flattens this distinction is what the report explicitly warns against.
- **Crediting Pingala with the explicit triangle.** The problem that generates it is in Pingala's domain, but the explicit triangular arrangement is most clearly in [Halayudha](../people/halayudha.md)'s 10th century commentary.
- **Treating the Meru-prastara as a "discovery" of binomial coefficients.** The tradition is reasoning about meter — a practical problem — and the binomial-coefficient structure emerges as the natural bookkeeping device. This is a significant contribution to combinatorial thinking, but not a context-free "discovery."
