## What it is

The **Vedangas** (literally "limbs of the Veda") are the six auxiliary sciences that the classical tradition recognized as necessary for preserving, pronouncing, interpreting, and applying the Vedic corpus. They are not part of the Vedas proper (which are Shruti); they are Smriti-level technical disciplines that serve Shruti. The standard list is:

1. **Shiksha** — phonetics and pronunciation
2. **Chandas** — prosody and metrical analysis
3. **Vyakarana** — grammar
4. **Nirukta** — etymology and semantic explanation
5. **Kalpa** — ritual procedure and law (including the Shrauta, Grhya, and Dharma Sutras)
6. **Jyotisha** — astronomy and calendrics for timing rituals

The framing "limbs of the Veda" treats the Vedic corpus as a body and these six disciplines as the joints that let the body function in practice. Each Vedanga produced its own foundational sutras, commentaries, and later scholarly tradition, and several went on to become major independent knowledge systems.

## Where each sits in the broader tradition

- [**Chandas**](../shastras/prosody-combinatorics.md) — the prosody tradition of Pingala, whose Chandas Sutra developed into the mathematical-combinatorial treatment of metre (Meru-prastara, Fibonacci-like recurrence). Foundational text: [Chandas Sutra (Pingala)](../texts/chandas-sutra.md).
- [**Vyakarana**](../shastras/vyakarana-grammar.md) — the grammatical tradition of Panini, whose Ashtadhyayi became the tradition's most technically sophisticated achievement and a peer to modern formal grammar. Foundational text: [Ashtadhyayi (Panini)](../texts/ashtadhyayi.md).
- [**Jyotisha**](../shastras/jyotisha-astronomy.md) — the astronomy/calendrics tradition that began with the Vedanga Jyotisha (calendrical) and developed into the Siddhanta-era planetary models of the Surya Siddhanta. Foundational text: [Vedanga Jyotisha](../texts/vedanga-jyotisha.md).
- **Shiksha** *(not yet seeded)* — multiple Shiksha texts survive; phonetics is an early articulation of articulatory phonology. The Taittiriya Upanishad's phonetic chapter is a famous early passage. The tradition is a direct ancestor of modern Sanskrit phonetic analysis.
- **Nirukta** *(not yet seeded)* — Yaska's Nirukta (c. 500–700 BCE) is the foundational etymological text, preceding Panini. It attempts to explain the meanings of difficult Vedic words by proposing etymologies, and it contains early reflections on word classification (noun, verb, preposition, particle).
- **Kalpa** *(not yet seeded)* — the Kalpa Sutras comprise three families: Shrauta Sutras (public ritual), Grhya Sutras (household ritual), and Dharma Sutras (law and conduct). The Dharma Sutras later grew into the Dharmashastra tradition (Manusmriti, Yajnavalkya Smriti, etc.).

## Why the framework matters

- **It explains why grammar, prosody, and astronomy are "sacred" sciences in classical India.** They are auxiliaries to the Veda: Vyakarana preserves pronunciation, Chandas preserves metre, Jyotisha fixes ritual timing, Shiksha fixes phonetic value, Nirukta preserves meaning, Kalpa prescribes ritual procedure. The classification names their function, not their content.
- **It is a coherent intellectual infrastructure for an oral-ritual tradition.** The Vedas were transmitted orally for centuries before being written down, and the Vedangas are exactly the disciplines a civilization committed to oral preservation would need: accurate pronunciation, accurate metre, accurate timing, accurate meaning, accurate procedure.
- **Several Vedangas outgrew their auxiliary function.** Vyakarana under Panini becomes a formal derivational system that modern linguistics and computer science still study. Jyotisha under the Siddhanta tradition becomes a mathematical astronomy with quantitative models. Chandas under Pingala becomes a combinatorial mathematics. The Vedanga origin is the point of departure, not the ceiling.

## Category errors to avoid

- **"The Vedangas are themselves the Vedas."** No. The Vedas (Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda, Atharvaveda) are Shruti — the four foundational compilations. The Vedangas are Smriti-level auxiliary sciences that serve them.
- **"The Vedangas are 'just' religious."** Grammar, prosody, and astronomy are sciences with objective technical content that can be and has been evaluated on its own merits, even when their origin is ritual preservation. Panini's grammar is a formal system; its ritual motivation does not disqualify it.
- **"Jyotisha = astrology."** Jyotisha covers both astronomy (positional, calendrical, observational) and astrology (natal charts, predictions). The Vedanga Jyotisha stratum is almost entirely astronomical-calendrical. Astrological applications developed later. See [Jyotisha (Astronomy & Timekeeping)](../shastras/jyotisha-astronomy.md).
