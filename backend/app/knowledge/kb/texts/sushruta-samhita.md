## What it is

The Sushruta Samhita is the second canonical compendium of classical Ayurveda, distinctive for its extensive treatment of **surgery (shalya tantra)**. Where the Charaka Samhita's strongest content is internal medicine and clinical reasoning, the Sushruta Samhita is the tradition's core surgical text, including reconstructive techniques, anatomical descriptions based on cadaver study, and surgical instrumentation.

## Textual history

Like the Charaka Samhita, the received text is a redacted tradition rather than a single-author composition. Traditional attribution names Sushruta as a teacher in a surgical lineage; the extant text is a later redaction of teaching material that passed through multiple hands. Widely circulated English translations (such as the **Bhishagratna** translation tradition, 1907–1916) are historically important but should be tagged as *early translation* and cross-checked with modern scholarship.

## Dating debate

- Earliest surgical content: arguably 1st millennium BCE
- Received text as we have it: roughly 4th–5th c. CE redaction
- As with Charaka, treat the dating as a layered question

## Structure

- Six sections (sthanas):
  1. **Sutrasthana** — fundamentals, surgical instruments, fracture management
  2. **Nidanasthana** — diagnosis of surgical diseases
  3. **Sharirasthana** — anatomy and embryology, including cadaver-based dissection procedures
  4. **Chikitsasthana** — surgical and medical therapeutics
  5. **Kalpasthana** — toxicology (poisons and antidotes)
  6. **Uttara Tantra** — additional specialties (ophthalmology, ENT, pediatrics, psychiatry)

## Key passages

- **Surgical instruments**: the extensive catalog of instrument types
- **Rhinoplasty and reconstructive procedures**: the most famous surgical descriptions, widely cited in the history of plastic surgery
- **Anatomical descriptions**: based on instructions for cadaver study
- **Eye surgery (cataract couching)**: described in the Uttara Tantra
- **Toxicology section**: classification of poisons and antidote protocols

## Modern translations worth citing

- **Bhishagratna's early 20th-century English translation** — tag as *early translation*; widely cited but superseded in specific sections by modern scholarship
- **Wujastyk's The Roots of Ayurveda** — the best modern scholarly entry point for selected passages
- **P.V. Sharma's Chaukhamba edition** — standard scholarly Sanskrit-English reference
