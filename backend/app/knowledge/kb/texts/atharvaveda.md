## What it is

The Atharvaveda is the fourth Veda — a collection of hymns, incantations, and formulas addressing everyday concerns: healing, protection, cursing, blessing, household rites, social relationships. Where the Rigveda is predominantly high-liturgical and the Samaveda melodic, the Atharvaveda is where the *lived texture* of Vedic culture shows up. It is also the primary early source for what Kenneth Zysk calls "medicine in the Veda" — a religio-medical tradition that precedes and sits beneath classical Ayurveda.

## Textual history

The Atharvaveda circulates in two surviving recensions: **Shaunaka** (more complete, the standard printed recension) and **Paippalada** (partially rediscovered from manuscripts in Odisha and Kashmir in the 20th century, with significant scholarly attention in the last few decades). The two recensions overlap substantially but also diverge, which matters for textual claims.

## Dating debate

Generally placed later than the core Rigvedic composition phase, with dating sometimes given as ~1200–900 BCE. Individual hymns may be older or younger than the collection's finalization. As with the Rigveda, treat the dating as a scholarly problem rather than a settled fact.

## Structure

- 20 books (kandas)
- ~6,000 verses in the Shaunaka recension
- Arranged broadly by the length and type of hymn rather than by author-family as in the Rigveda

## Key passages

- Healing hymns against fever, jaundice, wounds, snakebite (Zysk's translations and commentary are the definitive scholarly entry point)
- Protective and household incantations
- Hymns addressing relationships, love, rivalry, kingship
- Philosophical hymns (later material) including the Rohita and Skambha hymns

## Modern translations worth citing

- **Kenneth Zysk, Medicine in the Veda** — not a full translation of the Atharvaveda, but the best scholarly treatment of its medical content, translating and contextualizing the relevant hymns
- **Maurice Bloomfield, Hymns of the Atharva-Veda, 1897** — tag as *early translation*; widely available, scholarly for its time
- Recent Paippalada recension work in specialized indological publications
