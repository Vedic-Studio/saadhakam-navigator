## What it is

The Chandas Sutra is the foundational text of Sanskrit prosody (chandas), traditionally attributed to **Pingala**. It classifies and enumerates metrical patterns of short (laghu) and long (guru) syllables. Its historical importance for mathematics is that the enumerative problem of prosody generated systematic combinatorial thinking: generating all patterns of length *n*, counting them, and working with the triangular arrangement of binomial coefficients later known as the Meru-prastara.

## Textual history

The received text is short and sutra-style (terse aphoristic verses). It was preserved and elaborated through a long commentarial tradition. The single most important commentary for the mathematical content is **Halayudha's Mrita-Sanjivani** (10th c. CE), which explicitly discusses the Meru-prastara and makes the combinatorial machinery visible. This means that a substantial portion of what people cite as "Pingala" is actually Pingala-as-read-through-Halayudha.

## Dating debate

Pingala is often dated to around the 3rd–2nd c. BCE on traditional grounds, but modern history of mathematics scholarship treats this as uncertain. The text could be earlier or later, and the mathematical content is read through later commentaries in any case.

## Structure

- Eight books (adhyayas) in the standard arrangement
- Classification of meters by syllable count and pattern
- Rules for generating patterns and counting them
- Later sections develop the enumerative machinery

## Key passages

- **Pratyaya rules** — the methods for listing, counting, and reasoning about the set of possible metrical patterns (prastara, nasta, uddista, lagakriya, sankhya, adhvayoga in Halayudha's elaboration)
- Rules for generating patterns of length *n* from patterns of length *n–1* — the recurrence that modern historians cite as an early articulation of combinatorial generation
- Verses later interpreted as articulating the Meru-prastara (triangular array of binomial coefficients) — though the explicit Meru-prastara discussion is primarily in Halayudha's commentary, not in Pingala's sutras alone
