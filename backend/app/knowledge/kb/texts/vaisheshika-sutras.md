## What it is

The Vaisheshika Sutras are the foundational sutra text of the Vaisheshika darshana, canonically attributed to **Kanada**. The work sets out the school's six ontological categories (padarthas), its atomist theory of composition (anu/paramanu), and its approach to perception and inference as means of knowledge. The tradition is classically paired with Nyaya (the logic/epistemology school) and eventually merges with it into combined Nyaya-Vaisheshika.

The sutras themselves are terse; the mature expression of the school is in **Prashastapada's Padartha-dharma-sangraha** (~6th c. CE), which functions as the standard exposition and is what most later tradition engages with.

## Textual history

The received text is the sutras themselves, but the school's intellectual life runs through a long commentarial tradition — Prashastapada's systematic treatment, Udayana's later work, and the full Nyaya-Vaisheshika synthesis of the medieval period. Reading the sutras alone without this later apparatus produces distorted readings.

## Dating debate

- **Traditional dating**: Kanada placed in the 2nd c. BCE or earlier
- **Modern scholarly dating**: the received sutras are often placed around the turn of the common era, possibly later
- Treat the dating as unsettled; there is no consensus precise enough to support clean priority claims relative to Greek atomism

## Structure

- Ten chapters (adhyayas)
- Covers the six padarthas, atomist theory of composition, theory of motion, perception and inference, and the self (atman)
- Terse sutra style requiring commentarial elaboration

## Key passages

- **Padartha enumeration**: the six categories — substance (dravya), quality (guna), action (karma), generality (samanya), particularity (vishesha), inherence (samavaya) — with a later seventh (abhava, non-existence) added
- **Atomism passages**: the articulation of anu/paramanu as the smallest indivisible units and the rules for their combination into dyads (dvyanuka) and triads (tryanuka) and larger composites
- **Theory of motion**
- **Perception and inference as pramanas**
