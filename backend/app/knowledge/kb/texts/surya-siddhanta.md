## What it is

Surya Siddhanta is a Sanskrit treatise in Indian astronomy and one of the foundational Siddhanta-phase texts. The content covers planetary positions, eclipses, calendrics, and astronomical instruments, within a geocentric computational framework using epicyclic and equant-like devices typical of classical siddhanta models.

For content purposes, the most important single move is to treat Surya Siddhanta as a **textual tradition with revisions**, not a single monolithic book from one date. The received text has been revised across centuries, and different parameters within it can reflect different epochs.

## Textual history

The text exists in multiple manuscripts. Modern scholarship (Burgess's 19th-century translation, Pingree's historical work, and contemporary treatments by Plofker and others) emphasizes that the received text likely underwent revisions between roughly the 4th century CE and the medieval period. Different versions of the text circulate, which is why any statement about "what Surya Siddhanta says" needs to specify the recension and the section.

## Dating debate

- **Received text is medieval in its current form**, even though the *tradition* is older.
- **Parameter-specific dating**: scholarly work identifies multiple "update epochs" for different astronomical parameters within the text. A calendrical epoch of ~580 CE is often cited; earlier and later epochs have also been argued for by various researchers.
- The clean "Surya Siddhanta was written in year X" claim is a compression of a much more complicated textual history.

## Structure

- 14 chapters (adhyayas)
- Mean motions of planets, true motions, eclipses, heliacal risings, moon's elevation, gnomon, armillary sphere, time measurement

## Key passages

- Mean planetary period tables
- Eclipse calculation methods
- Instrument descriptions (gnomon, water clock, armillary sphere)
- Discussions of Earth's shape and celestial coordinates

## Modern translations worth citing

- **Ebenezer Burgess, Translation of the Surya-Siddhanta, 1860** — the foundational English translation with extensive notes by an American missionary-scholar. Tag as *early translation*; still widely cited but should be cross-checked with modern history of science scholarship.
- **Plofker's treatment in Mathematics in India (2009)** — the best modern frame for interpreting Surya Siddhanta's place in Indian astronomy
- **Pingree's articles** — for cross-cultural textual history
