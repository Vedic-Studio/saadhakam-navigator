## What it is

Vedanga Jyotisha is one of the earliest surviving Indian texts focused on **calendrical astronomy** in service of ritual scheduling. It is traditionally attributed to **Lagadha**. The purpose is practical: fix the timing of Vedic rituals in a way that reconciles lunar and solar cycles, handles intercalation, and standardizes the calendar for religious use.

For content purposes, the critical move is to treat this as a **calendrical manual**, not a modern astronomy textbook. It calculates what it needs to calculate to support ritual timing; it does not articulate planetary models.

## Textual history

The text survives in two recensions — **Rk** and **Yajus** — associated with the Rigveda and Yajurveda schools respectively. The two recensions have significant overlap but also divergences, and the manuscript tradition has its own complications.

## Dating debate

This is one of the most actively debated dating questions in early Indian astronomy.

- One position: the astronomical phenomena described in the text correspond to much earlier epochs (sometimes millennia earlier than the usually cited composition date).
- Counter-position: what a text *describes* and when it was *composed/transmitted* are distinct questions. An astronomical reference can be preserved from a much earlier observation.
- Plofker and other mainstream historians treat the composition date as uncertain but typically cite ranges in the several centuries BCE.

Present this as a live scholarly problem, not a settled answer.

## Structure

A short text — on the order of several dozen verses in each recension. Content covers:

- Calendar of months and seasons
- Rules for intercalation
- Placement of nakshatras relative to ritual timing
- Duration of day/night at different times of year

## Key passages

- Verses describing the five-year **yuga** cycle used for calendrical calculation
- Intercalation rules
- Nakshatra-based markers for ritual timing
