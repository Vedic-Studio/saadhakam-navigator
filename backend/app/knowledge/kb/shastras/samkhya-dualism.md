## Territory

**Samkhya** (literally "enumeration" or "reckoning") is the oldest systematic philosophical darshana in the classical Indian tradition. Its distinctive contribution is a **radical dualism between consciousness (purusha) and matter/nature (prakriti)**, an **enumerative cosmology** of 25 principles (tattvas), and a **theory of the three gunas** (sattva, rajas, tamas) as the fundamental constituents of phenomenal existence. It is paired with Yoga as its practice-wing and supplies the metaphysical backbone for most of classical Indian philosophy of mind.

Samkhya is unusual among orthodox darshanas in being **formally atheistic** in its classical form: it does not require a creator God and explicitly argues against the coherence of such arguments. Yoga inherits Samkhya's metaphysics but adds Ishvara as a meditational focus.

## Textual strata

- [Samkhya Karika (Ishvarakrishna)](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — c. 350–450 CE, 72 verses; the oldest surviving systematic Samkhya text and the standard reference
- **Samkhya Sutras (attributed to Kapila)** *(not yet seeded)* — later text, not the original; a later compilation that bears the founder's name
- **Gaudapada Bhashya** *(not yet seeded)* — early commentary on the Samkhya Karika
- **Vachaspati Mishra's Tattva Kaumudi** *(not yet seeded)* — the standard Sanskrit commentary
- **Yuktidipika** *(not yet seeded)* — an important anonymous commentary that preserves much of the lost earlier Samkhya tradition

The sage **Kapila** is the traditional founder, placed in legendary antiquity. The surviving classical tradition runs through Ishvarakrishna rather than directly from Kapila, and much of the earliest Samkhya literature is lost.

## Core concepts

- **Purusha** (consciousness) — plural, inactive, pure witness-consciousness; not a single unified Brahman
- **Prakriti** (primal nature) — the single material principle from which the phenomenal world evolves
- **Tanmatras and mahabhutas** — subtle and gross elements produced by prakriti's evolution
- [Three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas)](../concepts/guna-theory.md) — the three strands whose combinations constitute all of prakriti
- **25 tattvas** — the full enumeration: purusha + prakriti + mahat/buddhi + ahamkara + manas + 5 tanmatras + 5 jnanendriyas + 5 karmendriyas + 5 mahabhutas
- **Kaivalya** — liberation as *isolation* of purusha from prakriti; the goal is not union but separation of consciousness from matter

## Live debates

- **Plural purushas or one purusha?** Classical Samkhya is adamant on pluralism: every being has a separate purusha. Later Samkhya under Vedanta influence sometimes softened this.
- **Is Samkhya theistic?** Classical Samkhya is formally atheistic; Yoga adds Ishvara; later Samkhya under Puranic/Bhakti influence adds devotional elements. The "original" posture is debated.
- **Relation to Buddhism**: Samkhya's pluralism of consciousnesses and its enumerative analysis share surface features with Abhidharma; scholars debate the direction of influence.
- **What became of Samkhya after Ishvarakrishna?** Samkhya largely disappears as a living darshana after the first millennium CE, absorbed into Yoga, Vedanta, and Puranic traditions — but its concepts saturate everything downstream.

## Common misreadings

- **"Samkhya is just dualism like Descartes."** The comparison is instructive but breaks: Samkhya's purusha is plural (one per being), inactive, and a pure witness; Cartesian minds are unified, active, and thinking substances. Different models.
- **"The 25 tattvas are a physics."** No — they are a metaphysical enumeration, not empirical claims about matter. Treating tanmatras as "subtle particles" or gunas as "forces" imports physics vocabulary the tradition does not use.
- **"Samkhya gunas are personality types."** The gunas-as-three-personality-types reading is a popular distortion. The gunas in Samkhya are cosmological principles that constitute all of prakriti, not just a typology of people. Personality applications are a later derivation.
- **"Samkhya is Hinduism's version of science."** Samkhya is a metaphysics of liberation with an enumerative method. Calling it "proto-science" overclaims the empirical ambition and underclaims the soteriological structure.

## Modern bridges

- **Philosophy of mind and the "hard problem"**: Samkhya's rigorous separation of consciousness (purusha) from everything else (including mind/buddhi, which is part of prakriti) is structurally close to some contemporary arguments about the irreducibility of phenomenal consciousness. The Samkhya move — treating even mental events as material — prefigures some modern panpsychist and neutral-monist positions. *Breaks because* Samkhya is a liberation metaphysics, not an explanatory theory of neural correlates.
- **Enactivism and embodied cognition**: Samkhya's treatment of buddhi, ahamkara, and manas as parts of prakriti (not consciousness proper) loosely parallels embodied-cognition treatments of cognitive processes as material-functional rather than phenomenally conscious. *Breaks because* the surrounding metaphysical machinery (gunas, tanmatras, kaivalya) has no modern analog.
- **Consciousness studies**: the purusha–prakriti split is the most influential classical Indian framework for treating consciousness as categorically distinct from cognition, and this maps onto live questions in contemporary consciousness studies about whether phenomenal experience can be fully explained in functional terms.
