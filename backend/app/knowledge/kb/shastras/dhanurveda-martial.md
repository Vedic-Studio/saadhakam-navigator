## Territory

Dhanurveda is a category of martial knowledge preserved in multiple textual forms rather than a single universally agreed "Vedic manual." Later classifications list it as an **Upaveda** (an applied branch of Vedic knowledge), alongside Ayurveda, Gandharvaveda (music), and Sthapatyaveda (architecture). The responsible framing is: (a) martial knowledge was a real shastric tradition with textual, pedagogical, and performative layers; (b) the surviving texts are often *later compilations preserving martial curricula*, not direct evidence of early Vedic-period military practice; (c) the ethical and strategic counterpart is Arthashastra and the dharmayuddha–kutayuddha debate.

## Textual strata

- **Dhanurveda sections embedded in the Agni Purana** — an encyclopedic Puranic compilation preserving significant martial material
- **Dhanurveda Samhita** (later compilations attributed to figures like Vasistha) — *not yet seeded*
- References scattered across epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata) and other Puranas
- **Vishnudharmottara Purana** — *not yet seeded*; another Puranic compilation with relevant material

## Key figures

- **Drona, Bhishma, Arjuna** — the paradigm archery-lineage figures from the Mahabharata, functioning as tradition-founding exemplars rather than historical authors
- **Kaushik Roy** *(not yet seeded)* — modern historian framing the long-running Indian debate on ethics of warfare

## Core concepts

- [Dharmayuddha vs Kutayuddha](../concepts/dharmayuddha-vs-kutayuddha.md) — just war vs pragmatic/strategic war; the frame that ties Dhanurveda to Arthashastra and dharmashastra

## Live debates

- **How much of the surviving Dhanurveda literature reflects early Vedic practice vs later compilation?** The honest answer is: mostly later compilation preserving older curricula, with unclear relationship to the earliest period.
- **Is Dhanurveda a unified tradition or a category label?** Modern scholarship tends to treat it more as a category than a unified corpus.
- **How does Dhanurveda relate to surviving Indian martial arts (kalarippayattu, gatka, etc.)?** Lineages are complex; direct textual descent is rarely provable.

## Common misreadings

- **"The Vedas contain a complete military manual."** No. Dhanurveda as a textual tradition is mostly preserved in later compilations, and treating it as "the Vedas" conflates genres.
- **"Dhanurveda describes lost advanced weapons technology."** Claims about astras (divine weapons) in epics are literary and theological, not straightforward historical descriptions of technology.
- **"India had no ethics of war."** The dharmayuddha debate is extensive and takes the ethical dimensions of warfare seriously; pragmatic/strategic (kutayuddha) considerations are the counterposition, not a dismissal of ethics.
- **"Dhanurveda is only about archery."** The archery root is in the name, but the category covers broader martial knowledge including formations, weapons categories, and training protocols.

## Modern bridges

- **Doctrine and ethics of war**: the dharmayuddha–kutayuddha debate has a recognizable descendant in modern just war theory. *Breaks because* the classical debate is embedded in a religio-legal framework (dharma) that modern just war theory operates without.
- **Martial pedagogy**: the tradition's emphasis on staged training, teacher lineage, and ritualized apprenticeship parallels modern martial arts pedagogy. *Breaks because* direct continuity to specific modern schools is rarely provable.
