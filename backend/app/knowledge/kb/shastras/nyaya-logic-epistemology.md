## Territory

**Nyaya** is one of the six classical darshanas of Indian philosophy. Its distinctive contribution is **systematic logic and epistemology**: a theory of the means of knowledge (pramanas), the structure of inference (anumana), the rules of debate, and the typology of fallacies. It is traditionally attributed to the sage **Gautama** (also called Akshapada) and its foundational text is the **Nyaya Sutras**.

Historically, Nyaya fuses with **Vaisheshika** (the ontology/atomism school) into a combined **Nyaya-Vaisheshika** tradition that together supplies logic, epistemology, and ontology as a single integrated system. Its later development in the 13th century produces **Navya-Nyaya** ("New Nyaya"), founded by **Gangesha**, which develops a highly technical analytical vocabulary regarded by many modern Indologists as a peer to medieval European scholastic logic.

## Core concepts

- [Pramana (means of knowledge)](../concepts/pramana-epistemology.md) — Nyaya canonically recognizes four: perception (pratyaksha), inference (anumana), comparison (upamana), and testimony (shabda)
- [Anumana (inference)](../concepts/anumana.md) — the Nyaya syllogism has a distinctive five-member structure (thesis, reason, example, application, conclusion) that differs meaningfully from the Aristotelian three-member syllogism
- [Padarthas (categories)](../concepts/padarthas.md) — the shared Nyaya-Vaisheshika ontological categories (substance, quality, action, universal, particular, inherence, absence)
- **Hetvabhasa (fallacies)** *(not yet seeded)* — Nyaya develops a detailed typology of reasoning fallacies
- **Debate theory (katha, vada, jalpa, vitanda)** *(not yet seeded)* — Nyaya classifies debate into honest philosophical dialogue, victory-oriented disputation, and mere refutation

## Live debates

- **Is the Nyaya five-member syllogism logically equivalent to the Aristotelian three-member syllogism?** Modern Indologists (Matilal, Ganeri) argue the Nyaya form has distinct epistemic concerns — particularly the role of the example (drshtanta) — that make it not a redundant restatement of the Aristotelian form.
- **Is Navya-Nyaya doing "first-order logic" in a technical sense?** Some modern scholars make strong comparisons; others caution that the comparison, while illuminating, should not collapse the very different surrounding concerns.
- **The Nyaya-Buddhist debate on anumana and apoha (exclusion) is a major philosophical lineage** in classical India, running for a thousand years and driving most of the technical development on both sides.

## Common misreadings

- **"Nyaya = Aristotelian logic in Sanskrit."** No. Nyaya shares structural concerns with Aristotelian logic (both care about valid inference) but develops them differently, with distinctive concerns about the role of examples, the epistemology of the reasoner, and the social practice of debate.
- **"Indian logic is mystical and non-formal."** No. Navya-Nyaya, especially from Gangesha onwards, is famously technical — modern analytic philosophers find it among the most rigorous and dry logical traditions in the premodern world.
- **"Nyaya is just Vaisheshika's logic arm."** The two traditions fused historically but had separate origins and distinct concerns. Nyaya's original distinctive contribution is epistemology and debate; Vaisheshika's is ontology.

## Modern bridges

- **Formal epistemology and philosophy of inference**: Nyaya's pramana typology (perception / inference / comparison / testimony) is a systematic epistemology with recognizable modern counterparts, and its careful treatment of testimony (shabda) as a distinct pramana is philosophically sophisticated in ways contemporary epistemologists have begun to rediscover. *Breaks because* Nyaya's surrounding concerns (the Vedic corpus, the soteriological role of knowledge) differ from modern analytic epistemology.
- **AI reasoning systems**: rule-based expert systems and some approaches to automated theorem proving have surface parallels to Nyaya's explicit inference structures. *Breaks because* Nyaya is a theory of *human* reasoning in debate, not an engineering discipline for mechanized inference.
- **Philosophy of language**: Nyaya's theory of meaning, reference, and universals is in direct dialogue with Buddhist nominalism (the apoha debate). *Breaks because* the conceptual vocabulary differs from the Frege-Russell tradition that modern philosophy of language inherits.
