## Territory

Indian medical tradition runs through two clearly distinguishable stages, and collapsing them produces most of the confusion in public discussion.

**Stage 1 — Vedic healing as religio-medical practice.** The Atharvaveda preserves hymns and practices for healing, protection, and removing affliction. It combines incantation, ritual, and herbal/nature-derived elements. This is not classical Ayurveda as a systematic medical science; it is an early record of a healing culture in which religion and medicine are not yet separated.

**Stage 2 — Classical Ayurveda as developed medical literature.** The Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, and later digests form a systematic tradition with doctrine (tridosha), clinical categories, surgical techniques, materia medica, and institutional apprenticeship. This is the stage that modern Ayurveda and its clinical integration with biomedicine descend from.

A serious content treatment names the stage, anchors in primary sources, and treats "ancient Indians knew X modern medicine" claims as individual cases to be decomposed rather than endorsed wholesale.

## Live debates

- **What is Prakriti classification measuring, biologically?** Preliminary research — including a Nature Scientific Reports genome-wide association study — looks for genetic correlates of Prakriti categories. Results are presented as preliminary and exploratory, not as validation of the full classical doctrine. This is a live research program.
- **How much of classical Ayurveda is efficacious when tested to modern evidence standards?** The WHO Traditional Medicine Strategy framework offers a responsible way to discuss this: integration is possible where safety, quality, and evidence thresholds are met.
- **When does "classical Ayurveda" actually crystallize?** Dating of Charaka and Sushruta is itself contested, with significant layers of redaction across centuries.

## Common misreadings

- **"Ayurveda = 5000-year-old science."** The tradition is old, but classical Ayurveda is a *textual* tradition whose dating is contested and whose earliest stratum (Atharvaveda healing) is not the same thing as the later clinical compendia.
- **"Dosha = modern biomarker."** Dosha is a classical explanatory framework for constitution and pathology, not a direct equivalent of a measurable biomarker. Studies that look for biological correlates should be cited as *investigating* the framework, not as having *validated* it.
- **"Sushruta performed plastic surgery."** There is genuine surgical description in the Sushruta Samhita that includes reconstructive techniques. The responsible claim is narrower than the popular one and should be stated with textual locators and scholarly context. *(This decomposition is in the "not yet seeded" claims backlog.)*
- **"All Ayurvedic remedies are safe because they are natural."** Modern integration explicitly addresses quality, contamination, and dosing concerns.

## Modern bridges

- **Phenotypic classification for personalized medicine**: the Prakriti framework is a phenotypic-classification approach that predates and parallels modern personalized-medicine ambitions. *Breaks because* modern personalized medicine aims for molecular and genetic specificity that Prakriti does not claim.
- **Herb-based drug discovery**: modern pharmacology has real examples of active compounds isolated from traditional Indian herbs. *Breaks because* isolating active compounds is not the same thing as validating a doctrine of three humors.
- **Whole-systems chronic-disease management**: the classical framework's attention to diet, sleep, activity, and mental state resonates with modern chronic-disease guidelines. *Breaks because* the causal models differ.
