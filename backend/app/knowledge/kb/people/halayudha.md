## One-line identity

A 10th century CE commentator on Pingala's Chandas Sutra whose Mrita-Sanjivani is the most important single commentary for the mathematical content of the Indian prosody tradition — particularly the explicit discussion of the Meru-prastara (the triangular array of binomial coefficients).

## Works

- **Mrita-Sanjivani** — commentary on [Pingala](pingala.md)'s [Chandas Sutra](../texts/chandas-sutra.md)

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The explicit and systematic discussion of the Meru-prastara as a triangular arrangement of binomial coefficients. When modern treatments speak of "Pingala's triangle," a significant portion of that content is actually Halayudha reading and elaborating Pingala.
- The pratyaya methods (prastara, nasta, uddista, lagakriya, sankhya, adhvayoga) for reasoning about sets of metrical patterns

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- *Inventing* the Meru-prastara — the triangular array can be traced through earlier prosodic thinking; Halayudha makes it explicit
- Originating the combinatorial tradition — he is preserving, systematizing, and making legible what preceded him
