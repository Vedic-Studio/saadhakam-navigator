## One-line identity

The systematizer of classical Yoga — the compiler of the [Yoga Sutras](../texts/yoga-sutras.md) that present ashtanga yoga and the theory of citta-vritti-nirodha as a unified philosophical-meditative framework.

## Life

Almost nothing is known about Patanjali's life with certainty. The received tradition identifies "Patanjali" as the author of the Yoga Sutras, and Indian tradition has sometimes further identified this Patanjali with **the Patanjali who wrote the Mahabhashya** (a major 2nd c. BCE commentary on Panini's Ashtadhyayi) — a conflation that most modern scholars reject. These are almost certainly two distinct persons separated by several centuries.

**Philipp Maas's 2006 critical study** argues that the Yoga Sutras and the Yoga Bhashya (traditionally attributed to a separate "Vyasa") are in fact **a single work by a single author** — Maas calls this composite work the **Patanjala Yogashastra** and places it in the 4th–5th c. CE. This is the current best scholarly position on the text, though it is not unanimously accepted. If Maas is right, then "Patanjali" was both author of the terse sutras and author of the detailed prose exposition that has traditionally been treated as a separate commentary.

## Works

- [Yoga Sutras](../texts/yoga-sutras.md) — 195 sutras in four chapters; the foundational text of classical Yoga
- **Yoga Bhashya** *(not yet seeded; on the Maas hypothesis, by the same author as the Sutras)* — the detailed prose exposition traditionally attributed to "Vyasa"

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The **systematic framework of ashtanga yoga** — the eight limbs: yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi
- The **definitional sutra** YS 1.2: "yogah citta-vritti-nirodhah" — "yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind"
- The concept of **samyama** (combined dharana-dhyana-samadhi on a single object) and the siddhis/vibhutis that arise from it
- The **Ishvara passages** (YS 1.23–29) — devotion to Ishvara as a means of samadhi; classical Yoga's theistic element that distinguishes it from Samkhya
- The framework of **kaivalya** (aloneness) as the final liberation

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Being the same person as Patanjali the grammarian.** The author of the Mahabhashya (2nd c. BCE commentary on Panini) and the author of the Yoga Sutras (4th–5th c. CE) are almost certainly different persons. Traditional conflation should not be taken as historical identity.
- **Inventing yoga.** Yoga as meditative practice predates Patanjali by many centuries (the Upanishads, the epics, and the Shramana traditions all contain yoga-like practices). Patanjali systematizes and codifies; he does not invent the practice.
- **Inventing modern postural yoga.** The asanas of modern postural yoga (Bharatanatyam-style poses, vinyasa flows, etc.) are substantially a 20th-century development, drawing on hatha yoga (which is itself later and distinct from classical Patanjali yoga) and on modern physical-culture movements. Mark Singleton's *Yoga Body* (2010) is the definitive scholarly account of this history.
- **Teaching a dualism that is identical to Samkhya.** Classical Yoga inherits most of Samkhya's metaphysics but adds Ishvara — Samkhya itself is classically non-theistic (or at most weakly theistic); Yoga is more clearly theistic.
