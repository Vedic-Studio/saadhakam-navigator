## One-line identity

The attributed author of the [Arthashastra](../texts/arthashastra.md) and — by classical tradition — the minister and strategist behind Chandragupta Maurya's rise, historically identified with the figures called Chanakya and Vishnugupta in various traditions.

## Works

- [Arthashastra](../texts/arthashastra.md) — the treatise on statecraft, economy, law, intelligence, and war

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The systematic treatment of statecraft as a technical discipline (economics, administration, law, intelligence, war)
- The structural role of espionage in governance (not as a side topic but as part of the administrative fabric)
- The mandala theory of interstate relations
- The saptanga framework for the constituents of the state

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Writing every word of the received Arthashastra in the 4th c. BCE.** The received text is a composite tradition with layers spanning roughly the 3rd c. BCE to the 3rd c. CE. "Kautilya wrote the Arthashastra" is a compressed statement about this tradition.
- **Being "the Indian Machiavelli."** The comparison is suggestive but misleading; the Arthashastra is far broader in scope than The Prince and operates in a different intellectual register.
- **Being identical with Chanakya in a confirmed historical sense.** The identification with Chanakya the minister of Chandragupta Maurya is traditional; historical confirmation is limited.
