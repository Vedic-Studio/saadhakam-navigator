## One-line identity

The traditional author of the [Mimamsa Sutras](../texts/mimamsa-sutras.md), the foundational text of the Purva Mimamsa darshana — the tradition of Vedic exegesis and ritual hermeneutics.

## Life

Almost nothing can be said with confidence about a historical Jaimini. The name appears in the Mimamsa tradition as the author of the Mimamsa Sutras and is also attached to a separate (and very different) Jaimini Bharata and a Jaimini Brahmana, though these attributions are traditional and probably do not reflect a single historical figure. Traditional Indian sources identify him as a disciple of Vyasa — the mythic compiler of the Vedas and the Mahabharata — which is a legendary rather than historical attribution. His traditional dating in the 4th–3rd c. BCE is possible but not established; the Mimamsa Sutras are a composite text whose core dating is itself disputed.

The historically responsible position is to treat "Jaimini" as the traditional designation attached to the foundational text of Purva Mimamsa, with the recognition that the text is composite and the individual author may be legendary.

## Works

- [Mimamsa Sutras](../texts/mimamsa-sutras.md) *(attributed)* — twelve chapters, the foundational systematic text of Purva Mimamsa

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The **foundational framing of Purva Mimamsa** as the inquiry into dharma (athato dharma-jijnasa)
- The definition of dharma as **codana-lakshana** — "that which is indicated by Vedic injunction"
- The sutric framework that Shabara, Kumarila, and Prabhakara would elaborate into a full philosophical system

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Being a single historical author.** The Mimamsa Sutras are composite and probably reflect generations of development within a school; the attribution to "Jaimini" is traditional.
- **Being the same person as the Jaimini of the Jaimini Bharata or the Jaimini Brahmana.** These attributions are traditional and do not establish historical identity.
- **Being a disciple of Vyasa in any historical sense.** The Vyasa-Jaimini relationship is a legendary-mythic framing of the transmission of tradition.
