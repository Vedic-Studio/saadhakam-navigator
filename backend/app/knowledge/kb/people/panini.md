## One-line identity

The composer of the [Ashtadhyayi](../texts/ashtadhyayi.md), the terse, derivational grammar of Classical Sanskrit that became the foundation of the entire vyakarana tradition.

## Works

- [Ashtadhyayi](../texts/ashtadhyayi.md) — ~4,000 sutras in eight chapters

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The derivational rule system of Sanskrit — rules, meta-rules, rule ordering, conflict resolution
- The use of anubandhas (diacritical markers in the metalanguage)
- The Shiva Sutras / pratyahara system for compactly representing phonological classes
- The karaka framework for semantic-grammatical roles
- The formalization-of-grammar claim (see [Panini as formal grammar](../claims/panini-formal-grammar-claim.md))

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- Inventing binary numbers, Backus-Naur Form, or programming languages in any historical sense. These are *structural analogies* modern computer scientists and linguists draw; they are not historical claims about what Panini was doing.
- Producing a *descriptive* grammar of Sanskrit in the modern linguistic sense. The Ashtadhyayi is a derivational system.
- Working alone. The system is incomprehensible without its commentarial tradition (Katyayana, Patanjali, Kashika, later sub-traditions).

## Sources on this person

- **Paul Kiparsky's articles** on Paninian rule ordering
- Work by Gerald Penn, John J. Lowe, and others on formal properties of Paninian systems
