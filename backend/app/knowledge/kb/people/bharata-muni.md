## One-line identity

The traditional and largely legendary author of the [Natya Shastra](../texts/natya-shastra.md) — the foundational text of classical Indian performing arts and the source of rasa theory.

## Life

Virtually nothing historical can be said about "Bharata Muni." The Natya Shastra presents its author as a sage (muni) who received the Natya Veda directly from Brahma and then taught it to his hundred sons and to the divine dancers (apsaras) — a mythic origin story rather than a historical biography. The name "Bharata" itself may be a generic term for an actor in classical Sanskrit usage, which would suggest that "Bharata Muni" is a traditional designation for the text's author meaning something like "the sage of the actors."

The Natya Shastra is a composite work whose core is generally placed between ~200 BCE and ~200 CE, with later additions continuing into the early first millennium CE. Any historical "Bharata" is at best one figure in a compositional process that extended over several centuries. The responsible historical position is to treat "Bharata Muni" as the traditional attribution attached to the received text, not as an attested historical individual.

Despite the legendary character of the attribution, the text's *influence* is unambiguously historical and enormous: the Natya Shastra shaped classical Indian performing arts, aesthetics, literary theory, and iconography for nearly two thousand years, and all classical Indian dance, music, and dramatic traditions look back to it as their theoretical root.

## Works

- [Natya Shastra](../texts/natya-shastra.md) *(attributed)* — 36 chapters, ~6,000 verses; the foundational text of classical Indian performing arts, stagecraft, dramatic theory, and rasa aesthetics

## What they are cited for in this KB

- Authorship attribution for the Natya Shastra
- The systematic framework of classical Indian **performing arts** — theatre, dance, music, stagecraft, aesthetics
- The foundational presentation of **rasa theory** — the eight classical rasas (shringara, hasya, karuna, raudra, vira, bhayanaka, bibhatsa, adbhuta) and the formula of their production from bhavas
- The **four modes of abhinaya** (angika, vachika, aharya, sattvika)
- The framework within which Abhinavagupta (~1000 CE) would develop the classical high point of Indian aesthetic theory

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Being a single historical author in the ordinary sense.** The Natya Shastra is composite; "Bharata Muni" is a traditional and possibly generic designation.
- **Inventing classical Indian dance or music.** The text systematizes an existing rich tradition of performance and musical practice; it is a theoretical consolidation, not the invention of the art.
- **Directly founding Bharatanatyam or any modern classical dance form.** Modern classical dance forms (Bharatanatyam, Odissi, Kuchipudi, Kathakali, etc.) trace themselves to the Natya Shastra's tradition but in their current forms are substantially 19th–20th century reconstructions and codifications, not continuous unbroken descents from a Bharata Muni ~2000 years ago.
