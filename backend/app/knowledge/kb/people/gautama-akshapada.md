## One-line identity

The traditional author of the [Nyaya Sutras](../texts/nyaya-sutras.md), the foundational text of the Nyaya darshana — classical Indian logic, epistemology, and debate theory.

## Life

Almost nothing can be said with confidence about a historical Gautama-Akshapada. The name "Gautama" is shared with the Buddha (Siddhartha Gautama) and several other traditional figures; the epithet "Akshapada" ("eye-footed") is traditional and its meaning is not entirely clear — later tradition offers folk etymologies. The Nyaya tradition distinguishes its founder from the Buddha clearly; the two are different persons.

The Nyaya Sutras are a composite text whose core dating is generally placed between ~200 BCE and ~200 CE, with later chapters possibly added in stages. "Gautama-Akshapada" is the traditional designation rather than an attested historical author in the ordinary biographical sense. Modern scholarship treats the attribution as a traditional anchor for the composite text, not as a historical biography.

The school Gautama founds is the Nyaya darshana, which develops continuously through Vatsyayana (Bhashya, ~5th c.), Uddyotakara (~6th c.), Vachaspati Mishra (~9th c.), Udayana (~11th c.), and Gangesha (13th c., the founder of Navya-Nyaya) — a continuous scholastic tradition of roughly 1,500 years.

## Works

- [Nyaya Sutras](../texts/nyaya-sutras.md) *(attributed)* — five books, the foundational systematic text of Nyaya

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The **foundational framing of Nyaya** around sixteen topics (padarthas) whose right understanding produces liberation
- The **four means of knowledge (pramanas)**: perception, inference, analogy, testimony
- The **five-membered inference schema**: thesis, reason, example, application, conclusion
- The **taxonomy of debate** (vada, jalpa, vitanda) and the **catalogue of fallacies** (hetvabhasa)

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Being a single historical author.** The Nyaya Sutras are composite; "Gautama-Akshapada" is a traditional designation.
- **Being the Buddha.** Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) and Gautama-Akshapada (the Nyaya founder) are different persons despite sharing the name "Gautama." Classical tradition is clear on this; popular confusion sometimes conflates them.
- **Anticipating all of modern logic.** Nyaya logic is genuinely sophisticated and some of its moves resemble modern formal logic, but the characterization of Nyaya as "Indian formal logic fully equivalent to modern first-order logic" overclaims. See [Nyaya first-order logic claim](../claims/nyaya-first-order-logic-claim.md).
