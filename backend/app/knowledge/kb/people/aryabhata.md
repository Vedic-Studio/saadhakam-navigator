## One-line identity

The author of the [Aryabhatiya](../texts/aryabhatiya.md) (499 CE), the first named classical Indian mathematician-astronomer whose work survives and one of the most consequential figures in the global history of mathematics.

## Life

Aryabhata tells us in the Kalakriya Pada of the Aryabhatiya that he composed the work at age 23 in the year Kaliyuga 3600 — corresponding to 499 CE. This gives his birth year as 476 CE, one of the very few firm birth dates for any premodern Indian thinker. He worked at **Kusumapura** (modern Patna/Pataliputra), which was then a major center of learning in the Gupta period. Beyond these bare facts almost nothing is known about his life — there is no biographical tradition, no anecdotes, no lineage information. What we have is the text and a few self-references in it.

## Works

- [Aryabhatiya](../texts/aryabhatiya.md) — 499 CE, 121 verses in four padas; the foundational text of the school
- **Arya-siddhanta** *(lost)* — a second astronomical work by Aryabhata that is referenced by later writers but has not survived

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The earliest surviving systematic exposition of the decimal place-value system in classical Indian mathematics
- The **earth-rotation passage** (Gola Pada 9) — the claim that the earth rotates on its axis, producing the apparent motion of the stars
- The **value of pi ≈ 3.1416** — remarkable precision, explicitly marked as approximate
- The earliest surviving **sine table** in Indian mathematics, and the recursive sine-difference formula that generates it
- The **kuttaka method** for solving linear indeterminate equations
- The founding of the Aryabhata school of astronomy, which runs from the 5th c. CE through the Kerala school in the 14th–17th c. CE

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Heliocentrism.** Aryabhata argued that the earth rotates on its axis (diurnal rotation), which is a distinct and weaker claim than heliocentrism. His planetary model is not heliocentric. The popular claim that "Aryabhata discovered heliocentrism a thousand years before Copernicus" conflates rotation with heliocentrism. See [Ancient heliocentrism claim](../claims/ancient-heliocentrism-claim.md).
- **Inventing zero.** The decimal place-value system and the use of zero as a placeholder were developing in Indian mathematics in the centuries around Aryabhata; he used the system expertly but did not "invent" it. See [Indian zero claim](../claims/indian-zero-claim.md).
- **Computing the year length to modern precision.** Aryabhata's parameters are remarkable for their time but are not accurate to modern observational standards; popular claims about "nine-decimal-place accuracy" usually misread the alphabetic-numeric encoding.
