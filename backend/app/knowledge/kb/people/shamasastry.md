## One-line identity

The librarian-scholar in Mysore who rediscovered a manuscript of the [Arthashastra](../texts/arthashastra.md) in **1905**, published the Sanskrit edition in 1909, and produced the first complete English translation in 1915 — turning what had been a largely lost text into a cornerstone of modern Indological scholarship.

## Works

- **Sanskrit edition of the Arthashastra (1909)**
- **English translation of Kautilya's Arthashastra (1915)** — tag as *early translation*; still widely cited, particularly for its explicit treatment of intelligence passages

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The rediscovery narrative itself — one of the genuinely dramatic episodes in modern Indology and a strong narrative hook for content about ancient Indian political thought
- The first accessible English rendering of the text, which fundamentally shaped 20th-century reception of classical Indian statecraft
- Explicit translation of passages on spies, counter-espionage, and the structural role of intelligence in governance

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- The modern reference translation — that is now **Patrick Olivelle's 2013 edition**. Cite Shamasastry for historical importance and for specific passages; cross-check with Olivelle and Kangle for up-to-date scholarship.
- Critical philological reconstruction of the layered composition history, which was done by later scholars
