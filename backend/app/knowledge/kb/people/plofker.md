## One-line identity

A historian of mathematics at Union College whose Mathematics in India: 500 BCE – 1800 CE is the standard modern reference for the history of Indian mathematics and astronomy in English.

## Works

- **Mathematics in India: 500 BCE – 1800 CE (Princeton 2009)** — the definitive single-volume modern synthesis
- Articles on Indian astronomy, Kerala school mathematics, and manuscript traditions

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The best modern scholarly frame for interpreting Indian mathematics and astronomy responsibly
- Careful treatment of what "accuracy" claims about classical Indian astronomy can and cannot support
- The bridge between Indological textual scholarship and the history of mathematics as a discipline
- Situating prosody (Pingala), combinatorics, and the Fibonacci attribution chain in their actual historical context

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- Sensationalized priority claims about Indian mathematics. Her work is careful, contextualized, and explicitly avoids the "India had everything first" framing.
- A complete translation of any single Sanskrit mathematical treatise — the book is a synthesis, not a translation volume
