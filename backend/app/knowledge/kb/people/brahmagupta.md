## One-line identity

The author of the [Brahmasphutasiddhanta](../texts/brahmasphutasiddhanta.md) (628 CE), whose explicit rules for the arithmetic of zero and negative numbers are a landmark in the global history of mathematics.

## Life

Brahmagupta says he composed the Brahmasphutasiddhanta in Shaka 550 (628 CE) at age 30, at **Bhillamala** (modern Bhinmal in Rajasthan), then the capital of the Chavda dynasty. He composed a later work, the **Khandakhadyaka**, in 665 CE. Beyond these self-statements in his texts, no detailed biographical tradition exists.

Brahmagupta's historical importance extends well beyond India: his works were **translated into Arabic** in the late 8th century (as the *Sindhind*) at the court of al-Mansur in Baghdad, and were one of the key channels through which Indian numerical notation and algebraic techniques reached the Islamic mathematical world and, eventually, Europe. Without the Brahmasphutasiddhanta's translation into Arabic, the global spread of the Hindu-Arabic numeral system would have taken a different route.

## Works

- [Brahmasphutasiddhanta](../texts/brahmasphutasiddhanta.md) — 628 CE; 24 chapters; includes the arithmetic rules for zero and negatives, Brahmagupta's theorem on cyclic quadrilaterals, the Brahmagupta-Fibonacci identity, and a complete astronomical system
- **Khandakhadyaka** — 665 CE; a later, more compact astronomical handbook (karana grantha) intended for practical calculation, based on Aryabhata's system rather than Brahmagupta's own

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The **earliest surviving systematic arithmetic of zero and negative numbers** (Brahmasphutasiddhanta Chapter 18) — the single most cited Brahmagupta contribution
- **Brahmagupta's theorem** — the area formula for cyclic quadrilaterals
- The **Brahmagupta-Fibonacci identity** — (a² + b²)(c² + d²) = (ac − bd)² + (ad + bc)²
- Sophisticated treatment of indeterminate quadratic equations (vargaprakriti, the ancestor of Pell's equation work)
- Transmission route: Brahmagupta's works into Arabic in the 8th c. CE → Islamic mathematics → Europe

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Inventing zero "out of nothing."** The decimal place-value system and zero-as-placeholder were developing in Indian mathematics over several centuries before Brahmagupta; his contribution is the *systematic arithmetic of zero as a number* alongside negatives, not the invention of zero as a notation. See [Indian zero claim](../claims/indian-zero-claim.md).
- **Correctly handling division by zero.** Brahmagupta's rule that 0/0 = 0 is incorrect. Bhaskara II in the 12th c. made a more careful (though still not fully correct) attempt to treat division by zero.
- **Being a heliocentrist.** Brahmagupta explicitly rejected Aryabhata's claim of earth rotation (in the polemical Chapter 11 of the Brahmasphutasiddhanta) and held to a standard stationary-earth view.
