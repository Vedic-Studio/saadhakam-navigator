## One-line identity

The prosodist traditionally credited with the [Chandas Sutra](../texts/chandas-sutra.md), the foundational text of Sanskrit prosody — and through it, the starting point of a long tradition of combinatorial thinking in India.

## Works

- [Chandas Sutra](../texts/chandas-sutra.md) — the sutras themselves

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The enumerative procedures for listing metrical patterns (prastara, sankhya, etc. as elaborated by Halayudha's commentary)
- The structurally-binary treatment of laghu/guru syllables
- The starting point of the Indian tradition of combinatorial reasoning that later develops into the Meru-prastara and the Virahanka–Hemachandra recurrence

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **"Inventing binary numbers."** The enumerative structure is binary-like; there is no positional binary number system in Pingala's sutras. See the [Pingala binary numbers claim](../claims/pingala-binary-numbers-claim.md) for the full decomposition.
- **"Inventing Fibonacci numbers."** The recurrence now called Fibonacci becomes explicit only in [Virahanka](virahanka.md) and crystallizes in [Hemachandra](hemachandra.md). See the [Fibonacci / Virahanka attribution claim](../claims/fibonacci-virahanka-attribution.md).
- **The Meru-prastara in its explicit triangular form** — that is primarily in [Halayudha's](halayudha.md) 10th c. commentary, not in Pingala's sutras themselves.
