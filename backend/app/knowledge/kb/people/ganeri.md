## One-line identity

One of the leading modern scholars of **classical and early modern Indian philosophy**, whose work has expanded the analytic engagement with Indian thought that B. K. Matilal began and whose monograph **The Lost Age of Reason** opened up the substantial 15th–17th century Sanskrit philosophical literature as a serious philosophical resource.

## Life

Jonardon Ganeri (b. 1963) was educated at Cambridge, completed his D.Phil. at Oxford under B. K. Matilal, and has held positions at King's College London, the University of Sussex, the University of New York (NYU and NYU Abu Dhabi), and is currently the Bimal K. Matilal Distinguished Professor of Philosophy at the University of Toronto. He was elected a Fellow of the British Academy in 2015 — the first philosopher working on Indian thought to receive this recognition — and has served as editor of the Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy (2017).

Ganeri's career can be thought of in three phases: first, technical work on Nyaya logic and philosophy of language in the Matilal tradition; second, the landmark historical project on early modern Indian philosophy (~1450–1700), arguing that a sophisticated intellectual culture was active in India in the early modern period and is not adequately captured by either "classical" or "colonial" categories; and third, broader philosophical and comparative work on self, attention, ethics, and the possibility of a cosmopolitan philosophy.

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The argument that **early modern Indian philosophy** (~1450–1700) is a distinct and sophisticated intellectual culture that should be taken seriously alongside early modern European philosophy
- Rigorous treatment of **Nyaya logic, semantics, and epistemology** in engagement with analytic philosophy
- The Matilal-to-Ganeri lineage of contemporary English-language scholarship on Indian philosophy
- Editing the **Oxford Handbook of Indian Philosophy** — currently the best single reference for the field
- The broader argument that philosophy is and always has been a cosmopolitan enterprise, not a European one to which "other" traditions were later added

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Discovering that Indian philosophy exists.** That's an absurd characterization; Indian philosophy has been an active field for centuries. Ganeri's contribution is to bring specific bodies of work (especially the early modern corpus) to wider philosophical attention and to make analytic engagement with them philosophically productive.
- **Being an apologist for Indian thought.** Ganeri is rigorously critical and his comparative work does not privilege Indian positions over Western ones.
- **Working alone.** He is the leading figure but works within a larger community of scholars (Siderits, Phillips, Chakrabarti, Dasti, Freschi, etc.) that has collectively transformed the field.
