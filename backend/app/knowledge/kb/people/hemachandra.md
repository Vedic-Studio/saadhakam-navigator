## One-line identity

A major 12th century Jain polymath and scholar whose prosodic work is the point at which the recurrence now called Fibonacci numbers crystallizes in the Indian tradition — roughly 50 years before Leonardo Fibonacci's Liber Abaci.

## Works

- Chando'nushasana and related prosodic treatises
- Extensive work beyond prosody in grammar, lexicography, philosophy, and Jain religious literature

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The explicit statement of the recurrence now named for Fibonacci in the context of enumerating Sanskrit meters
- The crystallization of a tradition that runs Pingala → Virahanka → Hemachandra
- See the [Fibonacci / Virahanka attribution claim](../claims/fibonacci-virahanka-attribution.md)

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- A general theory of Fibonacci-like sequences outside the prosodic domain
- Priority over Pingala and Virahanka, who are earlier in the attribution chain
