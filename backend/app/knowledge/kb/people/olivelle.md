## One-line identity

A major modern Indologist at the University of Texas at Austin, whose translations of the Upanishads, Manu's Code of Law, and — centrally for this KB — the Arthashastra are the current scholarly reference editions in English.

## Works

- **King, Governance, and Law in Ancient India (2013)** — the modern reference translation of the [Arthashastra](../texts/arthashastra.md)
- **The Early Upanisads (1998)**
- **Manu's Code of Law (2005)**
- Editions and translations of Dharmasutras and related texts

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The current reference English translation of the Arthashastra
- The synthesis of composition-history scholarship on the Arthashastra: treating the received text as a layered tradition rather than a single-author composition
- Modern scholarly framing of classical Indian law, governance, and dharmashastra literature
- The intellectual bridge between 20th-century editions (Kangle) and 21st-century scholarship

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- Discovering the Arthashastra — [Shamasastry](shamasastry.md) did that in 1905
- Being a traditional commentator in the classical sense; he is a modern academic working with the tools of philology, history, and religious studies
