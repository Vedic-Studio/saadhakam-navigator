## One-line identity

The 12th-century mathematician-astronomer whose four-book **Siddhanta Shiromani** is the culmination of the classical north-Indian tradition of ganita and whose arithmetic book [Lilavati](../texts/lilavati.md) is arguably the most widely used Indian mathematics textbook ever written.

## Life

Bhaskara II (distinct from the earlier 7th-century Bhaskara I, a commentator on the Aryabhatiya) states in the Siddhanta Shiromani that he was born in Shaka 1036 (1114 CE) and composed the work at age 36 (i.e., in 1150 CE). He worked in **Ujjain**, which was the most important center of Indian astronomy in the medieval period. His family was a line of Brahmin astronomers; his father was the astronomer Maheshvara. The apocryphal story that Lilavati was named after his daughter (who was prevented from marrying at the astrologically auspicious moment by an accident) is traditional but not historically established.

Bhaskara II is the dominant figure of the classical north-Indian mathematical tradition in the centuries between Brahmagupta (7th c.) and the late-medieval decline. After Bhaskara II, the center of mathematical creativity shifts to the Kerala school in the south.

## Works

The **Siddhanta Shiromani** ("crest-jewel of treatises") is Bhaskara II's great encyclopedic work, in four parts:
- [Lilavati](../texts/lilavati.md) — the arithmetic book; 13 chapters; the most-used of the four as a textbook
- **Bijaganita** *(not yet seeded)* — the algebra book; covers positive and negative numbers, unknowns, surds, linear and quadratic equations, and the **chakravala method** for solving the indeterminate equation x² − Ny² = 1 (Pell's equation)
- **Grahaganita** *(not yet seeded)* — the planetary-astronomy book
- **Goladhyaya** *(not yet seeded)* — the spherical-astronomy book; contains Bhaskara's discussion of instruments and observational methods

Also the **Karanakutuhala** (1183 CE), a practical astronomical handbook.

## What they are cited for in this KB

- The **chakravala method** for solving x² − Ny² = 1 — a sophisticated cyclic algorithm that is not matched in European mathematics until the 17th c. (Fermat, Euler, Lagrange). This is Bhaskara II's most technically remarkable contribution.
- The **Lilavati as canonical textbook** — a literary-pedagogical achievement as much as a mathematical one
- The attempt at a more careful treatment of **division by zero** (Bhaskara introduces the concept of khahara, "that whose divisor is zero," as "unchanging") — still not fully correct but a significant advance over Brahmagupta
- Early treatments of what would later be called **Rolle's theorem** and instantaneous velocity (in the Goladhyaya's discussion of planetary motion) — proto-calculus ideas that anticipate the Kerala school's fuller development

## What they are NOT to be credited with

- **Inventing calculus.** Bhaskara II's Goladhyaya contains genuinely proto-calculus reasoning about instantaneous motion, and he explicitly uses ideas like "the instantaneous velocity of a planet" that resemble the limit concept. These are important precursors, but Bhaskara II does not have the full differential/integral apparatus. The Kerala school in the 14th–16th c. goes substantially further; neither goes all the way to Newton-Leibniz calculus.
- **Solving division by zero.** Bhaskara II's khahara is an advance but is not a modern solution. The honest modern account is that division by zero is undefined for finite numbers.
- **Single-handedly founding Indian mathematics.** Bhaskara II is the culminating synthesizer of a long tradition running from Aryabhata through Brahmagupta; he is not the starting point.
