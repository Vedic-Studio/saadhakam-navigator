## Territory

Dvaita Vedanta asserts five real, eternal, and irreducible distinctions (pancha-bheda): between God and soul, God and matter, soul and soul, soul and matter, and matter and matter. No amount of spiritual practice or knowledge collapses these distinctions. Vishnu (identified with Brahman) is the sole independent reality (svatantra); everything else, souls and matter alike, is eternally dependent (paratantra). Liberation is not identity with God but the soul's eternal experience of bliss in proximity to Vishnu, granted by God's grace alone.

Within the Vedanta tradition, Dvaita occupies the position of maximum ontological realism. Where Advaita treats difference as illusory and Vishishtadvaita treats it as real but organic (body-of-God), Madhva insists difference is both real and irreducible. This has consequences: souls are graded by inherent capacity (svarupa-yogyata), and not all souls achieve liberation. Some are eternally bound, and some descend into darkness (andhatamas). This doctrine of graded souls is the most controversial claim in Dvaita and the one most likely to surprise Western readers expecting universal salvation.

## Lineage & Key Figures

- **Madhvacharya** (~13th c.) — founder. Brahma Sutra Bhashya, Anuvyakhyana, Tattva-sankhyana, Vishnu-tattva-vinirnaya. 37 works total.
- **Jayatirtha** (~14th c., "Tikacharya") — Nyaya Sudha (commentary on Anuvyakhyana), systematized Madhva's logic
- **Vyasatirtha** (~15th-16th c.) — Nyayamrita (direct attack on Advaita's Advaita Siddhi), Tatparya Chandrika
- **Raghavendra Swami** (~17th c.) — prolific commentator, popularizer of Dvaita, widely venerated
- **Vadirajatirtha** (~16th c.) — Yukti-mallika, poetic-philosophical defense of Dvaita

## Core Concepts

- **Pancha-bheda** (five differences) — five eternal, real distinctions that define the ontological structure of reality
- **Svatantra/Paratantra** (independent/dependent) — only Vishnu is self-sufficient; all else depends on him for existence and activity
- **Svarupa-yogyata** (inherent fitness) — souls have innate grades of spiritual capacity, not all equally disposed toward liberation
- **Vishnu-sarvottamatva** (Vishnu's supreme sovereignty) — Vishnu is categorically superior to all other beings, including other deities
- **Sakshi** (inner witness) — Vishnu as the direct perceiver within each soul, grounding all knowledge
- **Bhakti** (devotion) — the sole means of liberation, but effective only when Vishnu's grace activates the soul's latent fitness
- **Aparoksha-jnana** (direct knowledge) — unmediated perception of Vishnu, the culmination of devotional practice

## Common Misreadings

- Equating Dvaita with Western theistic dualism (Cartesian mind-body). Madhva's dualism is between God and everything else, not mind and matter.
- Assuming "dualism" means only two categories. Pancha-bheda posits five real distinctions, not a simple two-way split.
- Missing the doctrine of eternal damnation (andhatamas). Western audiences expect all Sanatan schools to teach universal liberation.
- Treating Madhva as merely anti-Shankara. Dvaita has its own positive metaphysics, epistemology, and exegetical system independent of polemical positioning.

## Internal Tensions

- Graded liberation: is the hierarchy of souls a function of inherent nature or accumulated karma? Interpretations vary.
- Relationship to Vaishnavism: Dvaita is theologically Vaishnava, but its realist epistemology differs from the devotional emphasis of Gaudiya and Sri Vaishnava traditions.
- Status of Vayu (the wind god, identified with Madhva himself): how central is the Vayu-avatara claim to the philosophy vs. the hagiographic tradition?
- Whether Madhva's reading of the Brahma Sutras reflects their original intent or a creative reinterpretation

## Modern Bridges

- Pancha-bheda resonates with the "irreducible complexity" argument: real distinctions cannot be explained away. Breaks because Madhva grounds distinction in theology, not biology.
- Svarupa-yogyata maps loosely onto trait psychology (innate dispositions). Breaks because Madhva's grading is soteriological, not behavioral.
- Vishnu-sarvottamatva parallels monotheistic claims of divine sovereignty. Breaks because Madhva's Vishnu is not a creator ex nihilo but the controller of eternally existent matter and souls.

## Adjacent Domains

- **advaita-vedanta** — the primary philosophical opponent. Vyasatirtha's Nyayamrita directly attacks Advaita Siddhi's defense of mithya.
- **vishishtadvaita** — both affirm real souls and real God, but Dvaita rejects the body-of-God (sharira-shariri) model as collapsing difference.
- **vaishnavism-bhakti** — Dvaita provides the theological backbone for Haridasa devotional traditions in Karnataka.
