## Territory

Samkhya and Yoga are paired systems (often called "theory and practice") that share a common metaphysical framework but diverge on method and emphasis. Samkhya, attributed to the sage Kapila, establishes a strict dualism between consciousness (Purusha) and matter (Prakriti). Purusha is pure awareness, inactive, plural (each being has its own Purusha). Prakriti is unconscious, active, and the source of all mental and physical phenomena through its three gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas). Suffering arises from Purusha's false identification with Prakriti's modifications. Liberation (kaivalya) is Purusha recognizing its own nature as pure witness, separate from all experience.

Yoga, as codified by Patanjali in the Yoga Sutras, takes this Samkhya metaphysics and builds a practical method: the cessation of mental fluctuations (chitta-vrtti-nirodhah). Patanjali's eight-limbed (ashtanga) system moves from ethical restraints through posture, breath, and sense withdrawal to the inner practices of concentration, meditation, and absorption (samadhi). Writers must distinguish Patanjali's Yoga (a philosophical system about consciousness) from modern postural yoga (a 20th-century physical practice). They share the word "yoga" but operate at different levels of concern.

## Lineage & Key Figures

- **Kapila** (traditional founder of Samkhya) — attributed with the original enumeration of 25 tattvas. Historicity debated; referenced in Bhagavad Gita and Puranas.
- **Ishvara Krishna** (~4th-5th c.) — Samkhya Karika, the definitive systematic text of classical Samkhya. 72 verses.
- **Patanjali** (~2nd c. BCE to 4th c. CE, date debated) — Yoga Sutras, 196 aphorisms. The foundational text of classical Yoga.
- **Vyasa** (commentator, ~4th-5th c.) — Yoga Bhashya, the oldest and most authoritative commentary on the Yoga Sutras.
- **Vachaspati Mishra** (~9th c.) — Tattva Kaumudi (on Samkhya Karika), Tattva Vaisharadi (on Yoga Bhashya). Bridged Samkhya-Yoga with Advaita.

## Core Concepts

- **Purusha** (consciousness/witness) — pure awareness, inactive, uninvolved in change. Each individual has a distinct Purusha.
- **Prakriti** (primordial matter) — unconscious, dynamic, the root cause of all mental and physical phenomena through guna interaction
- **Gunas** (sattva, rajas, tamas) — three qualities or strands of Prakriti. Sattva (clarity/lightness), rajas (activity/agitation), tamas (inertia/heaviness). All manifest phenomena are guna combinations.
- **Chitta-vrtti-nirodhah** (cessation of mental modifications) — Patanjali's definition of yoga. Not emptiness but the stilling of Prakriti's mental activity so Purusha can see itself.
- **Kaivalya** (isolation/freedom) — liberation as Purusha's permanent disentanglement from Prakriti. Not union, but the end of false identification.
- **Ashtanga** (eight limbs) — yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi. Sequential discipline from external conduct to internal absorption.
- **Viveka-khyati** (discriminative discernment) — the sustained cognitive clarity that distinguishes Purusha from Prakriti, the proximate cause of liberation

## Common Misreadings

- Equating yoga with physical postures (asana). Asana is one of eight limbs, and in Patanjali means "stable, comfortable seat for meditation," not a workout.
- Treating Samkhya as atheistic in the Western sense. Classical Samkhya has no creator God, but this is not a rejection of the sacred; Purusha itself is the transcendent.
- Confusing Patanjali's chitta-vrtti-nirodhah with thought suppression. Nirodhah is cessation through discriminative insight, not forceful stoppage.
- Assuming kaivalya means cosmic union. It means the opposite: Purusha's recognition of its radical separateness from all mental content.

## Internal Tensions

- Does classical Samkhya have a place for Ishvara (God)? Patanjali introduces Ishvara-pranidhana (devotion to God), but Samkhya Karika does not. The relationship between the two systems on this point is genuinely unresolved.
- Plurality of Purushas: if each being has a separate Purusha, what explains the apparent uniformity of consciousness? Other schools (Advaita, Kashmir Shaivism) consider this the weakest point of Samkhya.
- Status of Patanjali's siddhis (supernatural powers, Vibhuti Pada): legitimate attainments on the path or distractions? The text itself warns they can become obstacles.
- Whether Yoga Sutras represent a unified philosophical system or a compilation of different practice traditions (kriya yoga, ashtanga yoga, Ishvara-centered yoga)

## Modern Bridges

- Chitta-vrtti-nirodhah maps onto noise-canceling headphones for consciousness: removing distortion to hear the signal. Breaks because the "signal" (Purusha) is not a content of experience but the experiencer itself.
- Purusha/Prakriti parallels the observer-observed split in quantum mechanics. Breaks because Samkhya's dualism is permanent and ontological, not a measurement artifact.
- Guna theory resonates with personality trait models (Big Five). Breaks because gunas are cosmological forces constituting all matter, not psychological descriptors.
- Ashtanga's sequential structure parallels progressive training programs. Breaks because the limbs are not merely sequential but interpenetrating; ethical foundation (yama) and absorption (samadhi) inform each other.

## Adjacent Domains

- **advaita-vedanta** — Advaita borrows Samkhya's analytical categories but collapses the Purusha-Prakriti dualism into non-dual Brahman. Vachaspati Mishra commented on both.
- **kashmir-shaivism** — absorbs Samkhya's tattvas (expanding from 25 to 36) but rejects the dualism: Shiva is both consciousness and its creative expression.
- **vedic-ritual** — Samkhya's analytical method provides philosophical grounding for the Vedic cosmological framework, though Mimamsa and Samkhya have different epistemological commitments.
