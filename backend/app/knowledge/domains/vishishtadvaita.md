## Territory

Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism) holds that Brahman is real, the world is real, and individual souls are real. All three are eternally distinct yet inseparable. Brahman is Narayana/Vishnu, who possesses infinite auspicious qualities and is the inner controller (antaryamin) of all souls and matter. Souls (chit) and matter (achit) are the body of Brahman, real but dependent. This makes Vishishtadvaita the middle position between Advaita's radical non-dualism and Dvaita's strict dualism.

The school's philosophical project is to affirm the reality of devotion (bhakti), the devotee, and the divine beloved as ontologically grounded, not provisional. Liberation is not the erasure of individuality but eternal, conscious communion with Narayana in Vaikuntha. Two paths lead there: bhakti yoga (sustained meditative devotion) and prapatti (complete self-surrender), with prapatti available to all regardless of caste, gender, or qualification. This makes Vishishtadvaita both philosophically rigorous and soteriologically egalitarian.

## Lineage & Key Figures

- **Nathamuni** (~10th c.) — systematized the Alvars' Tamil hymns, established the liturgical tradition
- **Yamunacharya** (~11th c.) — Siddhitraya, laid philosophical groundwork for Ramanuja
- **Ramanuja** (~11th-12th c.) — Sri Bhashya (commentary on Brahma Sutras), Gita Bhashya, Vedarthasangraha. Central figure.
- **Vedanta Desika** (~13th-14th c.) — Shatadushani (100 refutations of Advaita), leader of the Vadakalai (northern) sub-school
- **Pillai Lokacharya** (~13th c.) — Srivachana Bhushana, leader of the Tenkalai (southern) sub-school
- **Alvars** (~6th-9th c.) — 12 Tamil poet-saints whose Divya Prabandham hymns are treated as equivalent to the Vedas (ubhaya vedanta)

## Core Concepts

- **Vishishta-advaita** (qualified non-dualism) — Brahman is one, but internally differentiated by real attributes, souls, and matter
- **Sharira-shariri** (body-soul relation) — souls and matter are the body of Brahman; Brahman is their inner self
- **Prapatti** (self-surrender) — complete surrender to Narayana as an independent path to liberation, not requiring yogic qualification
- **Ubhaya Vedanta** (dual Vedanta) — both Sanskrit Vedas and Tamil Divya Prabandham are scriptural authority
- **Chit** (conscious souls) — eternally real, atomic in size, dependent on Brahman but never identical with Brahman
- **Achit** (unconscious matter) — real, undergoes transformation (parinama), forms the material body of Brahman
- **Aprithak-siddhi** (inseparability) — souls and matter cannot exist independently of Brahman, though they are not identical with Brahman

## Common Misreadings

- Treating Vishishtadvaita as "Advaita with minor qualifications." The disagreement is fundamental: the world and souls are real, not mithya.
- Assuming prapatti is just emotional surrender. It has a precise theological structure with six components (shadanga prapatti).
- Missing the Tamil dimension. The Alvars' devotional poetry carries scriptural weight equal to the Sanskrit Vedas in this tradition.
- Reducing it to "temple Hinduism." Ramanuja's philosophical arguments against Advaita (in Sri Bhashya) are among the most rigorous in Sanatan philosophy.

## Internal Tensions

- Vadakalai vs. Tenkalai split: does liberation require the devotee's effort (monkey model, Vadakalai) or only God's grace (cat model, Tenkalai)?
- Status of Divya Prabandham: equal to or subordinate to Sanskrit shruti?
- Whether prapatti replaces bhakti yoga or supplements it for those unable to practice bhakti
- Caste and access: Ramanuja's egalitarian intent vs. historical institutional restrictions

## Modern Bridges

- Sharira-shariri maps onto emergent systems thinking: parts are real, the whole is more than their sum. Breaks because Brahman is the intentional controller, not an emergent property.
- Prapatti resonates with "letting go" in therapy and recovery frameworks. Breaks because prapatti targets a specific personal God, not a psychological state.
- Ubhaya Vedanta (dual scripture) parallels multilingual legal systems. Breaks because the Tamil texts carry devotional and experiential authority, not just doctrinal equivalence.

## Adjacent Domains

- **advaita-vedanta** — both interpret Brahma Sutras but disagree on whether souls and matter are real. Ramanuja's Sri Bhashya is a systematic rebuttal of Shankara.
- **dvaita-vedanta** — both affirm real distinction between God and souls. Dvaita pushes further: five eternal, unbridgeable differences.
- **vaishnavism-bhakti** — Vishishtadvaita provides the theological framework; bhakti traditions provide the devotional practice and literature.
