## Territory

Advaita Vedanta is the non-dual school of Vedanta, grounded in the claim that Brahman (pure consciousness without qualities) is the sole reality, and that the experienced world of multiplicity is mithya: neither fully real nor fully unreal, but a superimposition (adhyasa) on Brahman. The individual self (jiva) is not a fragment of Brahman but is Brahman, obscured by beginningless ignorance (avidya). Liberation (moksha) is not an event or attainment but the removal of this ignorance through knowledge (jnana), specifically the recognition stated in the mahavakyas: "tat tvam asi" (you are that).

Within the broader Sanatan tradition, Advaita is the most philosophically aggressive school. It subsumes rival positions (Samkhya dualism, Nyaya realism, Purva Mimamsa ritualism) by treating them as provisional truths valid only at the transactional (vyavaharika) level. Writers covering Advaita must distinguish three orders of reality: paramarthika (absolute), vyavaharika (transactional), and pratibhasika (illusory, like a dream or mirage). Collapsing these into a flat "the world is an illusion" statement is the single most common distortion.

## Lineage & Key Figures

- **Gaudapada** (~6th-7th c.) — authored Mandukya Karika, first systematic exposition of ajativada (non-origination)
- **Adi Shankaracharya** (~8th c.) — composed bhashyas on Prasthanatrayi (Brahma Sutras, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita), Vivekachudamani, Upadeshasahasri. Founded four mathas.
- **Suresvara** (~8th c.) — direct disciple of Shankara, wrote Naishkarmya Siddhi. Championed the avidya-as-Brahman-locus view.
- **Padmapada** (~8th c.) — direct disciple, wrote Panchapadika. Held avidya's locus is the jiva, not Brahman.
- **Mandana Mishra** (~8th c.) — rival-turned-associate of Shankara, Brahmasiddhi. Bridge figure between Purva Mimamsa and Vedanta.
- **Vidyaranya** (~14th c.) — Panchadashi, systematized Advaita for accessibility. Head of Sringeri matha.

## Core Concepts

- **Brahman** (the absolute) — infinite, attributeless consciousness; the only independently real existent
- **Atman** (the self) — the witnessing awareness in each being, identical with Brahman
- **Mithya** (dependent reality) — the world is not non-existent but has no independent existence apart from Brahman
- **Avidya/Maya** (ignorance/creative power) — the beginningless condition that projects multiplicity onto Brahman
- **Adhyasa** (superimposition) — the cognitive error of attributing properties of the not-self to the self and vice versa
- **Mahavakyas** (great sentences) — Upanishadic declarations of identity, e.g., "tat tvam asi," "aham brahmasmi"
- **Viveka** (discrimination) — the capacity to distinguish the real (nitya) from the transient (anitya)
- **Moksha** (liberation) — not a future state but the present recognition that bondage was always illusory

## Common Misreadings

- "The world is an illusion" conflates mithya with tuccha (absolute non-existence like a hare's horn). Mithya means dependent, not fake.
- Treating Advaita as nihilism or world-denial. Shankara explicitly defends the validity of worldly activity within vyavaharika.
- Equating Advaita with Buddhism. Shankara argued extensively against Madhyamaka and Vijnanavada positions, despite surface similarities.
- Assuming Advaita rejects devotion. Shankara composed devotional hymns (Bhaja Govindam, Soundarya Lahari) and treated bhakti as a preparatory discipline.

## Internal Tensions

- Locus of avidya: does ignorance belong to Brahman or to the jiva? Suresvara vs. Padmapada traditions disagree.
- Role of Ishvara (personal God): is Ishvara a concession to the transactional level, or does saguna Brahman have independent theological weight?
- Jivan-mukti vs. videha-mukti: can liberation be fully realized while embodied, or only at death?
- Relationship between sruti (scripture) and anubhava (direct experience): which is the final pramana (means of knowledge)?

## Modern Bridges

- Mithya maps onto simulation theory: the world is "real within the simulation" but not independently real. Breaks because mithya has no external programmer.
- Adhyasa parallels cognitive science's constructive perception: the brain imposes structure on raw data. Breaks because Advaita's witness (sakshi) is non-computational.
- Viveka resonates with metacognition research: the ability to observe one's own thinking. Breaks because viveka aims at ontological discrimination, not better thinking strategies.
- "Consciousness is fundamental" aligns with panpsychism and David Chalmers' hard problem framing. Breaks because Advaita's consciousness is singular and attributeless, not a property distributed across matter.

## Adjacent Domains

- **vishishtadvaita** — agrees on Brahman's reality but insists Brahman is qualified by real souls and matter. The central dispute: is difference real or apparent?
- **dvaita-vedanta** — rejects identity of jiva and Brahman entirely. Madhva's pancha-bheda is the direct antithesis of Advaita's abheda.
- **yoga-samkhya** — Advaita borrows Samkhya's analytical categories but collapses its purusha-prakriti dualism into non-dual Brahman.
- **kashmir-shaivism** — both are non-dual, but Kashmir Shaivism treats the world as a real expression of Shiva's freedom, not as mithya.
