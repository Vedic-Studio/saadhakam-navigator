# Sadhaka Transposition Playbook

The adaptation layer. How every mechanic from BAM and TRP maps to Sanatan Dharma. What to keep. What to transform. What to reject outright. The concept-map of Sanatan analogues. The anti-pattern firewall.

Cross-reference: [sources/bam-analysis.md](sources/bam-analysis.md) §13, §14; [sources/trp-analysis.md](sources/trp-analysis.md) §16, §17.

---

## 1. What the Sadhaka book is, and is not

### It is
- A curated anthology of ~80–120 short pieces, each standalone, binding into a worldview by adjacency.
- A book about Sanātana Dharma as a living, scope-explicit metaphysics — not about "Hinduism" as religion, heritage, or identity marker.
- A book written for readers who have already sensed that mainstream packagings of the tradition (secular-liberal, pop-Vedanta, wellness-yoga, nationalist, academic) all miss the mark, and are ready to receive the tradition in its own register.
- A second-stage conversion asset, downstream of a public front-end (opensadhaka.com) that produces the first contact.
- A book designed to be read in two weeks, re-read annually, and kept on a nightstand rather than a shelf.

### It is not
- A manifesto for any political, nationalist, or ideological project.
- A polemic against other religions or against secularism.
- A textbook or introduction-to-Hinduism volume.
- A self-help or wellness book dressed in Sanskrit vocabulary.
- A re-translation of śruti — the śruti is already translated; this book quotes it and points at it.
- A book that requires the reader to already be Hindu, Indian, or previously-Sanskrit-literate. It is written so a serious reader of any background can enter and practice.

## 2. The transposition matrix

Every major mechanic from the source books, mapped. **KEEP** = port with minor adjustment. **TRANSFORM** = port the mechanic, substantially rebuild the content. **REJECT** = structural or ethical incompatibility.

### Architecture
| Source mechanic | Sadhaka disposition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Anthology of short self-contained posts | KEEP | 80–120 posts, same cell-independence principle |
| Four-Part structure with mythic-poetic titles | KEEP | Four Parts: The Veil / The Ground / The Path / The Silence |
| Numbered sections continuous across Parts (BAM) | TRANSFORM | Titled posts rather than numbered; titles do voice work |
| Variable length, fractal rhythm | KEEP | 30% short / 50% mid / 18% long / 2% pillar |
| Spiral sequencing around 8–12 core concepts | KEEP | 10–12 Sanatan concept-axes (māyā, ātman, dharma, etc.) |
| Close with release, not crescendo | KEEP | Final post is a śānti-register exhale |
| Heavy TOC with descriptive chapter names | REJECT | The book announces itself; no explanatory TOC |

### Voice
| Source mechanic | Sadhaka disposition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Single narrator with register-swing (BAM) | TRANSFORM | Named author as curator; four named voices inside the book |
| Multi-author anthology (TRP) | TRANSFORM | Four voice archetypes played by the same author (+ optional named contributors for Sādhaka voice) |
| Broken-English pidgin affectation (BAM) | REJECT | Instead: sūtra-terse construction; Sanskrit unglossed after first use |
| Anonymous prophet persona (BAM) | REJECT | Named-author-as-curator; the book's voice is the tradition's, not the author's |
| Second-person contract throughout | KEEP | "You" as default; "I" only in Sādhaka confessionals |
| Strategist / Confessor / Satirist / Authority voice-stack (TRP) | KEEP | Ācārya / Sādhaka / Satirist / Imported Voice |

### Rhetoric
| Source mechanic | Sadhaka disposition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Direct address + accusing rhetorical questions | KEEP | Unchanged — these are Sanskrit-sūtra-native moves |
| Mythic analogy as evidence (one-gesture pluck) | KEEP | Puranic / itihāsa gestures replace Greek/Roman |
| Negation-then-reveal | KEEP | Unchanged |
| Invocation / prayer-form closure | KEEP | Sanskrit half-verse or mantra as closure |
| Second-person sensory walk | KEEP | Indian landscape, temple detail, ghat, Kedarnath |
| Broken syntax for effect | TRANSFORM | Replace BAM pidgin with sūtra-terse English |
| Catalog under a slogan | KEEP | Unchanged — high pull-quote value |
| Totalizing claims without hedge | TRANSFORM | Preserve the rhythm; scope with *vyāvahārika / pāramārthika* |
| Intentional misspellings (BAM) | REJECT | Signals internet-of-2018 and dates the book |
| Ridicule-as-refutation | KEEP w/ limits | One Satirist post per 8; target patterns not persons |

### Conviction
| Source mechanic | Sadhaka disposition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Non-rational proof stack (seven mechanisms) | KEEP | All seven available; tradition provides superior grounding for each |
| Appeal to the ancient as untampered | KEEP | *Śruti* is the native form of this appeal |
| Appeal to nature / visible fact | KEEP | Indian landscape and fauna |
| Appeal to aesthetic disgust | KEEP w/ limits | Target patterns only, never persons |
| Appeal to body / instinct | KEEP | *Anubhava* and *smaraṇa* are the tradition's native forms |
| Appeal to hidden knowledge | KEEP | *Adhikāra*, sampradāya-secrecy, and mahāvākya-withholding |
| Revelation / vision | KEEP | *Śruti as apauruṣeya* |
| Testimony (TRP) | KEEP | Sādhaka-voice confessionals, plus hagiography (Ramana, Tulsidas, Mira) |
| Ridicule | KEEP w/ strict limits | See above |

### Edgy-idea handling
| Source mechanic | Sadhaka disposition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-empt objection and mock it (BAM) | TRANSFORM | Pre-empt and take seriously; show tradition already scoped it |
| Complicity framing ("you know what I mean") | TRANSFORM | Soften to "perhaps you have felt…" to avoid coercion |
| Concession-then-flip | KEEP | Unchanged |
| Wrap in myth so it reads archaic-poetic | KEEP | Puranic wrapping |
| Invoke reader's body as evidence | KEEP | Unchanged |
| Distance via named source | KEEP | Śruti > smṛti > ācārya-bhāṣya stack visible on page |
| "Your doubt is the programming" (TRP) | REJECT | Replace with *neti-neti* + anubhava-first invitation |
| In-group slurs | REJECT | Absolute ban — no coinages of the "bugman" type |

### Assimilation
| Source mechanic | Sadhaka disposition | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Insider lexicon as membership token | KEEP | ~30 Sanskrit terms, no English substitution |
| Aphoristic density target | KEEP | One pull-quote-worthy line per 300–500 words |
| Second-person contract | KEEP | See Voice |
| Variable length + spiral sequencing | KEEP | See Architecture |
| The unsaid as authority | KEEP | *Adhikāra* and mahāvākya-withholding are native |
| Close with release | KEEP | See Architecture |
| Book as second-stage conversion asset | KEEP | Site is front door; book is what you graduate to |
| Political / ideological coding | REJECT | Book must not read as left-coded or right-coded signal |

## 3. The concept-map — Sanatan analogues for source-book patterns

For every recurring pattern in BAM or TRP, the Sanatan tradition has a native concept or structure. Mapping these gives drafters a ready table.

### Existential diagnosis
| Source pattern | Sanatan analogue |
|---|---|
| "Iron Prison" / the modern cage (BAM) | *māyā*; specifically *avidyā* as the mechanism |
| "Yeast-life" — survival-only metabolism (BAM) | *tamoguṇa* when dominant; lack of *viveka* |
| "Bugman" — domesticated ressentiment-driven modern | the unreflective *saṁsāri*; specifically a man in whom *vairāgya* has not arisen |
| "Unplugging" — painful recognition of reality (TRP) | *viveka* arising; *śravaṇa* at its first bite |
| "Sexual marketplace" — nature of relations | *pravṛtti-mārga* vs *nivṛtti-mārga*; the scoped role of kāma within the four puruṣārthas |
| "Frame" — composure, self-control (TRP) | *sthira-sukham āsanam* applied beyond physical sitting; *drsthā* (the witness) held steady |

### Aspirational / high-type
| Source pattern | Sanatan analogue |
|---|---|
| "Lion-life" / mastery of space (BAM) | *sattva* ascending; *jīvanmukta* |
| "Warrior" / "pirate" — the free man (BAM) | *sādhaka*, specifically on the *nivṛtti-mārga* |
| "Alpha" (TRP) | inadequate mapping; Sanatan has no single-axis status hierarchy. Closest: the one in whom *śraddhā* and *abhyāsa* have integrated. Often better to simply not transpose this pattern |
| Bronze Age Greek aristocracy (BAM) | no map; the tradition's own heroic register is in *Rāmāyaṇa*, *Mahābhārata*, and the lives of ācāryas |

### Practice / path
| Source pattern | Sanatan analogue |
|---|---|
| "Sun and steel" / body discipline (BAM) | *āsana* as sthira-sukham; not as physical-training primary |
| "Game" (TRP) | inadequate; the closest transposition is *sādhana* itself — but this should not be transposed from TRP because TRP's framing is instrumental-seduction, the opposite of sādhana's frame |
| "Monk mode" (TRP) | *brahmacarya* in its original scope; not as abstinence primarily, as an alignment of energies |
| "Iron Rules" / axioms (TRP) | *sūtras*; the tradition has a thousand years of practice with this form |

### Transmission
| Source pattern | Sanatan analogue |
|---|---|
| Reading list / sidebar (TRP) | *pāṭha* — the graduated curriculum of a paramparā |
| Curated voices of the community (TRP) | *ācārya-paramparā*; the book treats itself as a small entry into this larger lineage |
| Anonymous frog informants (BAM) | no direct map, but the tradition's *gupta-vidyā* — secret knowledge transmitted privately — is structurally similar |
| Reader as potential initiate | *adhikārin* — the one fit for the teaching |

### Diagnosis of the modern spiritual marketplace
The tradition's own internal critique — across a thousand years — has been sharper than anything in BAM or TRP. Drafters should read widely in this critique before satirizing the contemporary marketplace. Primary sources for the Satirist voice:
- Bhāgavata's critiques of performative religion
- Ramakrishna's parables about hypocritical sādhus
- Vivekananda's polemics against temple-commerce and pandit-complacency
- Nisargadatta's flat dismissals of seekers who had not yet asked the first real question
- Ramana's gentleness-with-bite when asked meditation-technique questions

The Satirist voice inherits this lineage. It is not new; it is the tradition's own.

## 4. Anti-patterns (the firewall)

Things that must never appear in the book, regardless of their presence in the source books.

### Content anti-patterns
- **In-group slurs or coinages that tar a type of person.** "Bugman," "cuck," "womyn" (BAM); crude anatomical references (TRP). The pattern-naming mechanic is preserved; these specific words are not.
- **Misogyny in any form.** BAM's structural positioning of women as viragoes / prizes / drainers is incompatible with a tradition whose cosmology places Śakti at the center. A Sanatan book that imported this would fail on the first review; it would also be theologically wrong.
- **Racial or ethnic reductionism.** BAM's intermittent racialized shorthand. Absolute ban.
- **Political coding.** The book must not be readable as a left- or right-coded signal. The reader's politics are irrelevant to the book's business. Specific exclusions: no references to contemporary Indian political parties, no Western political terminology (woke, MAGA, feminist, progressive, etc.), no contemporary political figures named.
- **Cruelty for its own sake.** BAM's Caligula-celebrations. The intensity is preserved; the sadism is pruned.
- **Chronology denialism / Fomenko-style trolling.** BAM's occasional flex. The Sadhaka book's authority depends on its KB claims being scoped and true.
- **Manipulative capture moves.** TRP's "your doubt is the programming" trap. Replaced by *neti-neti*.
- **Reference classes that will date.** "Frogs," "NEET," "swole-left" (BAM); TRP's internal forum references. The Sadhaka book references the classical tradition, the natural world, and the body — none of which decay.
- **Overreliance on pop-evolutionary-psychology or one-axis social theory.** TRP's dominant mode. The tradition's own multi-axis machinery (the guṇas, the āśramas, the puruṣārthas, the yogas) provides vastly more explanatory power without the reductionism.
- **Fabricated confessionals.** If the author cannot supply from their own life, delegate to real contributors. Never invent.
- **Performative edginess.** Edge is an effect of precision, not a goal. If a passage reads as edgy-for-the-sake-of-reaching-contrarian-readers, cut it.

### Craft anti-patterns
- **Em dash overuse.** BAM's signature is an anti-pattern here.
- **Exclamations as full stops.** BAM uses them structurally; the Sadhaka book uses them rarely (once per Part, at most).
- **ALL-CAPS slogans.** Reserved for one use — the dedication invocation. Nowhere else.
- **Academic footnotes in the body.** Citation lives at the back or in KB claim-slugs.
- **Glossing Sanskrit terms after their first appearance.** Once defined, the term is trusted.
- **Two same-voice posts in a row.** Breaks the rotation.
- **Two same-length posts in a row (mid- or long-).** Breaks the fractal rhythm.
- **Summaries at post-end.** "In this post we have seen…" — signals textbook, destroys tone.

## 5. Scope-qualifier protocol

Every strong claim in the book is scoped. This is the book's native alternative to BAM's totalizing claims and TRP's weaponized certainty.

### The two primary scope tags
- **Vyāvahārika** — "from the transactional / empirical standpoint"
- **Pāramārthika** — "from the ultimate / absolute standpoint"

### Additional scope tags available
- **Specific darśana**: "In the Advaitic reading…" / "In Kashmir Shaiva…" / "In Śrīvaiṣṇava…" / "In Sāṅkhya…"
- **Specific ācārya**: "Shankara, on this, says…" / "Ramanuja's commentary…" / "Madhva's reading…"
- **Specific stage of sādhana**: "For the beginner, this is sufficient to know…" / "Once abhyāsa is stable, this second move becomes available…"
- **Specific practice tradition**: "The Vedantic approach…" / "The Tantric approach…" / "The Bhakti approach…"

### Drafting rule
Any claim that could be pull-quoted with a strong single-sentence form must also have a scope tag somewhere in its surrounding paragraph. The pull-quote stays punchy; the scope sits adjacent, available to the reader who goes looking.

> *"The witness is free. [pull-quote lands]*
> *This is said from the pāramārthika standpoint. From the vyāvahārika standpoint, you are the actor and the chooser, and this does not contradict the first claim. These are two floors of the same house."*

The sentence stays sticky. The scope stays honest.

## 6. The authority stack (on every claim)

Every major claim in the book rides on a visible authority stack. Inspired by TRP's Schopenhauer → William James → living author pattern, but with the tradition's native machinery.

### Default four-layer stack
1. **Śruti** — a Vedic / Upaniṣadic citation (even a half-verse)
2. **Smṛti** — a Gītā, Yoga-sūtra, or purāṇic passage
3. **Ācārya-bhāṣya** — Shankara, Ramanuja, Madhva, Abhinavagupta, Vallabha, Vijñānabhikṣu
4. **Living-teacher gloss** — Ramakrishna, Vivekananda, Ramana, Aurobindo, Nisargadatta, Sivananda

Not every post uses all four. Pillar posts use all four. Mid-length posts use two or three.

### Why this matters
- The claim arrives as a three-thousand-year chain, not a single voice's hot take.
- A reader who disagrees is disagreeing with the tradition, not the author. The author is the medium.
- The staircase is also the reader's reading list. Every claim points at what to read next.

### Craft rules
- **The stack is visible on the page.** Quote at least one layer in-text; reference the others by name.
- **The living-teacher voice does not out-rank the śruti.** The ordering from śruti downward is the tradition's own; the book respects it.
- **Do not invent the stack.** If a claim cannot actually be traced through the four layers, it is not a claim the book makes.
- **When layers disagree among themselves, show the disagreement.** The tradition is not monolithic. Shankara and Ramanuja disagree; the book's job is often to show them disagreeing with precision, not to pick a winner.

## 7. The "would the tradition recognize this?" test

Before finalizing any major claim, imagine Ramana, Nisargadatta, Ramakrishna, Vivekananda, Shankara, Abhinavagupta — any of the tradition's recognized voices — reading the passage. Would they say:

- "Yes, this is what we have been saying." → Good.
- "This is one angle; I would add…" → Good; add the scope qualifier.
- "This is a distortion." → Cut or rewrite.
- "This is a contemporary projection in Sanskrit costume." → Cut.

This test is harsher than it sounds. Much contemporary Sanatan writing fails it. The book's credibility depends on passing it.

## 8. Editorial hierarchy

When in conflict, prioritize in this order:
1. **The tradition's scope and precision.** If a move would flatten the tradition, abandon the move.
2. **The reader's dignity.** If a technique works on the reader but dis-respects them, abandon the technique.
3. **Aphoristic density / pull-quote engineering.** Important, but not at the cost of (1) or (2).
4. **Assimilation mechanics.** Important, but not at the cost of (1), (2), or (3).
5. **Shareability on social platforms.** Valuable, but downstream of all the above.

A book that fails the top priority to pass the bottom one is not a cult-favorite; it is content.

## 9. Voice-swap exercise (for drafters)

Any post that feels flat can be diagnosed by swapping its voice:

- If it reads as **Ācārya** now and feels flat — try drafting it as **Sādhaka** (a confessional). Does it get cost?
- If it reads as **Sādhaka** and feels flat — try drafting the same claim as an **Imported Voice** deep-dive (quote + gloss). Does the quote carry weight the confessional was asking the reader to provide?
- If it reads as **Satirist** and feels flat — the post is probably not Satirist material; try drafting it as **Ācārya** and see if the tone survives.
- If the post braids voices and feels overstuffed — strip to one dominant voice; let the others appear only as one-paragraph support.

## 10. What "cult-favorite" means for this book

A successful Sadhaka book is one where:
- Readers copy specific lines to their group chats.
- Readers who have never met recognize each other by quoting a Sanskrit term from the book's lexicon.
- A percentage of readers return to the book annually.
- The book is the most-recommended asset when a reader is asked "where should I start with Sanātana Dharma?" — and the recommender is someone whose judgment is trusted.
- The book is not visible on best-seller lists but is consistently over-indexed in serious readers' libraries.
- The book's language enters the reader's own speech and thought.

None of these outcomes require compromising the tradition. All of them are produced by the mechanics in this package, applied well, over 80–120 posts, by a drafter with patience.
