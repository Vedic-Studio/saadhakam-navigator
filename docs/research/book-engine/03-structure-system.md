# Structure System

Architecture at four scales: whole book → parts → posts/sections → paragraphs. Why anthology form beats monograph. Fractal rhythm. Spiral sequencing.

Cross-reference: [sources/bam-analysis.md](sources/bam-analysis.md) §1, §10; [sources/trp-analysis.md](sources/trp-analysis.md) §1, §14.

---

## 1. Whole-book architecture

### The anthology decision
Both cult sources are anthologies, even when they pretend otherwise.
- TRP Sidebar is explicitly curated posts from many authors.
- BAM is nominally one book by one author, but internally it reads as 77 short sections, each a standalone piece the author clearly could have published separately.

**The lesson**: a book whose cells survive independently travels better than a book whose argument is load-bearing across chapters. A reader who opens to page 147 of a monograph needs the previous 146 pages; a reader who opens to post 42 of an anthology is already in a complete unit.

### Sadhaka book target
- **80–120 pieces total** (the TRP Sidebar's range, slightly expanded)
- **Four Parts** with mythic-poetic titles (BAM's move). Proposed shape of the arc:
  1. *The Veil* — diagnosis of the modern condition; why nothing has landed; the specific modern ways Sanatan has been sold to the reader as something else.
  2. *The Ground* — cosmology, core darśanic terms, the śruti-smṛti-ācārya stack. What the tradition actually is, and how it scopes its own claims.
  3. *The Path* — sādhana, abhyāsa–vairāgya, the four yogas, the puruṣārthas, the daily architecture. What changes when you begin.
  4. *The Silence* — the end-state, mokṣa and its neighbours, the difference between saying and being. Exits into release.
- **10–30 pieces per Part**, with the interior count varying by Part (longest Part is typically Part Three / the path).

### Why four Parts, not three or five
- Four gives you rising-falling rhythm with a final exhale. (Three is too short; five diffuses.)
- Four matches existing fourfold schemes from the tradition — *puruṣārthas, āśramas, varnas, yugas, Vedas, avasthās (jāgrat-svapna-suṣupti-turīya)*. Any Part can be internally organized around one of these without forcing it.
- Four Parts also matches the TRP arc (opening framework → theory → practice → de-escalation) and lets the spiral sequencing work cleanly (every concept returns once per Part, four encounters total).

### Front matter
- No elaborate TOC. BAM has none in the scanned copy; TRP uses title-pages only for each post.
- A one-page **"About this book"** in the curator's voice: here is how to read it, here are the four voices, here is the advice on re-reading annually. Four paragraphs maximum.
- A one-page **dedication** that does voice-work: a Sanskrit half-verse or invocation (*"Victory to the gods"*-equivalent — *"aum namo bhagavate vāsudevāya"*, or some line whose weight the book will earn).
- **No preface.** Open with post #1 — a framework post that names something the reader already half-knows.

### Back matter
- A **glossary** post at the end (replicating the one in the early part, expanded) — Sanskrit terms with one-line definitions. Reader flips to this as an anchor on re-read.
- A **reading order suggestion** — two or three paths through the book: "first-time reader," "reader in difficulty," "reader revisiting after a year." TRP effectively does this by post-adjacency; we can be explicit.
- **No acknowledgments, no author bio in the book itself.** These live online. Book stays clean.

## 2. Part-level architecture

Each Part has a mythic-poetic title (not *"Chapter 1: Introduction to Cosmology"*). The title carries the Part's argument compressed.

**Opening move of a Part**: one short piece (200–500 words) in Ācārya voice that frames the Part. Names what it will show. Short, oracular, sets register.

**Closing move of a Part**: one short piece (200–500 words) — often in Imported Voice — that releases the tension built in the Part and hands the reader off to the next. Never a summary. Always a gesture forward.

**Interior sequencing**: see §4 below — "spiral sequencing" — which operates at the book level. Inside a Part, the interior ~20 posts cycle through the voice rotation (Ācārya / Sādhaka / Satirist / Imported Voice), the mood palette, and the length-rhythm.

## 3. Post-level (section-level) architecture

Each post is self-contained. A post survives without its neighbours.

### Length distribution (target)
| Length | % of total | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Very short (150–500 words) | 30% | Aphoristic codas, single-image pieces, sūtra-verse posts, short Satirist sketches |
| Mid-length (500–1500 words) | 50% | Standard Ācārya concept-primers, most Sādhaka confessionals, ordinary Satirist pieces |
| Long (1500–3000 words) | 18% | Pillar framework posts; deep puranic mini-essays; imported-voice deep-dives with extensive gloss |
| Pillar (3000–5000 words) | 2% | 2–4 pieces per Part. Multi-voice braided posts introducing a central concept (māyā, ātman, mokṣa) |

### The default post shape: Claim → Scene → Slogan
This is BAM's dominant unit and it works. Three beats inside a mid-length post:

**Beat 1 — Claim (10–20% of post):** one or two sentences of Ācārya-voice diagnosis or declaration. Does not justify itself. Lands.
> *"There are two kinds of silence. The silence between thoughts, and the silence beneath them. Do not confuse these."*

**Beat 2 — Scene (60–80% of post):** the body. A puranic vignette, a sādhaka confessional, a second-person sensory walk, a śloka with gloss, an observation of nature, a field report. The scene carries the proof-load. No argument in this section; only specifics.

**Beat 3 — Slogan (10–20% of post):** a compressed return. Often an imperative without argument; sometimes a Sanskrit half-verse; sometimes a rhetorical question that accuses. Designed to be pull-quoted.
> *"Sit at 4am. The second silence is already there. You only have to stop asking it to prove itself."*

Not every post uses this shape, but it's the default. Frameworks, confessionals, and pure-image pieces use their own templates (see [09-templates-kit.md](09-templates-kit.md)).

### Five post-shapes (BAM + TRP inventory)

1. **Framework post** — named construct with labelled sub-items. Ācārya voice. Leads with the skeleton, details the branches, justifies last or not at all.
2. **Confessional post** — Sādhaka voice. First-person failure → recognition → lesson → second-person invitation. AA-shaped.
3. **Claim → Scene → Slogan** — the default mid-length piece.
4. **Imported deep-dive** — extended śāstric passage with ācārya-bhāṣya and a living gloss. The tradition speaks; the curator explains minimally.
5. **Aphoristic coda** — 6–50 lines. One image, one declaration, one invitation. Functions as palate-cleanser between heavier posts.

[09-templates-kit.md](09-templates-kit.md) has the full nine templates including variants. These five are the load-bearing ones.

### Title conventions
- Short, memorable, often slightly enigmatic. BAM's sections are numbered without titles; TRP titles its posts but the titles are rarely descriptive — *"THE FOUR HORSEMEN OF MALE EMANCIPATION,"* *"WOMEN IN LOVE"*. Titles perform, they don't summarize.
- Mix of Sanskrit and English. *"Śraddhā is not belief."* *"The four veils."* *"Arjuna at the moment before."* *"What the peacock knows."*
- Occasionally a full sentence as title. *"Do not confuse the two silences."*

### Typography cues
- Sanskrit terms in italics on first appearance in a post; plain text thereafter.
- Imported blockquotes in italics with source attribution on the next line.
- Pull-quote-worthy lines get their own paragraph break — single-sentence paragraphs are legitimate structural moves.
- No ALL-CAPS except for the book's dedication page invocation. (BAM's all-caps-slogans are too loud for Sadhaka's register.)
- Em dashes sparingly (1–2 per post).

## 4. Spiral sequencing — book-level choreography

Neither BAM nor TRP is linear. Both spiral around 8–12 core ideas, visiting each from many angles.

### Sadhaka's core concepts (the spiral's axes)
Each concept gets 5–10 posts across the book — no single post exhausts the idea; each post lands one facet. Proposed axes:

1. **Māyā** — the modern condition, the veil, why the obvious is un-seen
2. **Ātman** — the seer; the *drg-drśya viveka*; the witness
3. **Brahman** — the ground; saguna and nirguna; the different darśanas' readings
4. **Dharma** — not ethics; the self-law; the svadharma question
5. **Karma** — across lives; as mechanism, not moralism; scoped correctly
6. **Mokṣa** — and its neighbours (jīvanmukta, videhamukta); what it is not
7. **Sādhana** — the daily architecture; the four yogas as modes not choices
8. **Bhakti** — distinguished from sentimentality; the six schools of bhakti; Tulsidas, Mira, Ramakrishna
9. **Jñāna** — the śravaṇa–manana–nididhyāsana ladder; Shankara's method
10. **Śraddhā** — the first move; not belief; the posture that makes sādhana possible
11. **Guru** — paramparā; distinguished from modern teacher-markets; the inner guru
12. **Rta / time** — yugas, the long clock; sandhyā as micro-rta; muhūrta

Every post is primarily about one of these axes. Secondary axes appear as ornament. Over 100 posts, each axis gets ~8 encounters, each from a different voice, mood, or scale (cosmic vs domestic).

### How to sequence the spiral
Not by axis-at-a-time (which would be monograph structure). Instead: **each axis is visited once per Part**. So the reader meets māyā four times across the book — once in Part One as diagnosis, once in Part Two as metaphysics, once in Part Three as what breaks under sādhana, once in Part Four as the lifted veil. Each encounter stands on its own, but the accumulating layering is the conversion engine.

### Fractal length-rhythm
Inside each Part, short and long posts alternate irregularly. Avoid runs of same-length posts. BAM §31 (8 lines) adjacent to §33 (150 lines) is the rhythm.

**Concrete heuristic for a sequence of 5 consecutive posts**: at least two length-categories should appear. Never five 1000-word posts in a row. The eye and attention both need the variance.

### Curated adjacency
Two adjacent posts should **disagree in register or voice** (sharpness into tenderness, Ācārya into Sādhaka, framework into aphorism). Don't queue two framework posts back to back. Don't queue two confessionals. The reader finishes one piece and is surprised by the next.

## 5. Paragraph-level architecture

Inside a single post, paragraphs are short and declarative.

- **Target paragraph length**: 40–120 words. Occasional 6–15 word paragraphs for punctuation.
- **Single-sentence paragraphs** are permitted and powerful — reserve for pull-quote-worthy lines.
- **Opening paragraph of any post** is 2–4 sentences. Lands fast. BAP's prologue opens with a denial ("This is not book of philosophy") in its first line; TRP's "Four Horsemen" post opens with the named taxonomy already in motion. Neither warms up.
- **Closing paragraph** is 1–3 sentences. Slogan, imperative, or Sanskrit half-verse.
- **Scene paragraphs** (in the middle of a Claim → Scene → Slogan post) can be longer — 80–150 words — to allow the sensory walk or the vignette to unfold. But always one beat per paragraph.

### Paragraph-break as pacing
Use paragraph breaks as rests. The reader's eye moves faster on short paragraphs; slower on long ones. Manipulate the pace deliberately. A 3-paragraph sequence of long-short-long creates anticipation. Long-long creates depth. Short-short-short creates urgency.

## 6. What to avoid structurally

- **Heavy TOC with explicit descriptive chapter names.** Kills the oracular effect. The book announces itself; a TOC that explains itself undermines the voice.
- **A long preface or introduction before post #1.** The book enters cold. BAP's prologue is not an introduction — it's a set-piece that immediately does voice-work.
- **Predictable length-pattern.** If the reader can guess how long the next post will be, they disengage.
- **Monograph argument load-bearing across posts.** Never let post 42 depend on the reader having internalized post 17. Each post stands.
- **Academic footnotes in the body.** The book is not an academic text. Citation lives at the end, as a source-table, or as KB claim-slugs in a separate volume. The body stays prophetic.
- **More than 120 total posts.** Above this, the reader feels it as a tome. The book must be completable in two weeks of daily reading, re-readable across a weekend.

## 7. Front-to-back length target

- **~60,000–90,000 words total**. Roughly 200–280 printed pages.
- This is BAM's range (shorter than TRP because single-author and denser).
- TRP is 370 pages but includes full imports (Vilar's chapter runs 65 pages inside it). Excluding imports, TRP's original essays total perhaps 180–220 pages.
- The Sadhaka book should feel slender. Slenderness signals confidence.

## 8. Production sequencing

When drafting, do not draft front-to-back. Draft by voice-batch and concept-batch:

1. Draft all 12 **pillar framework posts** first (two per Part, plus four cross-Part anchors). These establish the cosmology.
2. Draft the **Imported Voice deep-dives** next — these are mostly curation + gloss, and they anchor authority.
3. Draft **Sādhaka confessionals** — these are slow work, require real specificity; don't rush.
4. Draft **Claim → Scene → Slogan mid-length posts** in batches by concept axis.
5. Draft **Satirist posts** last — these should be reactive to the already-drafted register.
6. Draft **aphoristic codas** last-plus — these are curated from pull-quotes already surfaced in other posts.

Then sequence. Assemble into Parts. Run adjacency and length-rhythm checks. Rewrite openings and closings post-sequencing.

---

*Structure is downstream of voice but upstream of everything else. A book with great prose and wrong structure does not circulate.*
