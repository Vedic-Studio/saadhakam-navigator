## What the book is

A two-volume documentary survey arguing that the medieval Islamic invasions and rule in India (c. 650–1800 CE) caused systematic, theologically-motivated destruction of Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain places of worship across the subcontinent and adjacent regions. The book's evidentiary strength is that the bulk of its citations come from **Muslim chronicles, Muslim epigraphy, and archaeological survey reports** — not Hindu sources.

The book had a specific 1990–1993 political context (the Ramjanmabhoomi movement) and a polemical tone, especially in Vol II's theological sections. **Treat the evidentiary apparatus as primary research; treat the framing as one interpretation among several.**

## The evidentiary core (what to actually mine for Sadhaka)

### 1. The Maulana Abdul Hai list (Vol I, Ch 1) — strongest single citation

Maulana Hakim Sayid Abdul Hai (d. 1923), Rector of Darul-Ulum Nadwatul-Ulama in Lucknow, wrote *Hindustan Islami Ahad Mein* ("Hindustan under Islamic Rule"). The Urdu version published by the Nadwa in 1973 listed **seven mosques built on temple sites**:

1. **Quwwatul Islam Masjid**, Delhi (Qutb complex) — built by Qutubuddin Aibak from temple materials.
2. **Jami Masjid**, Qanauj — "built on the foundation of some Hindu Temple."
3. **Jami Masjid**, Etawah — on the bank of the Yamuna, on a Hindu temple site.
4. **Babri Masjid**, Ayodhya — "this mosque was constructed by Babar at Ayodhya which Hindus call the birth place of Ramchanderji ... On that very site Babar constructed this mosque in H. 963."
5. **Gyanvapi Masjid**, Benares — Aurangzeb, on the site of the Vishweshwar temple, using the same stones.
6. **Aurangzeb's second Benares mosque** — on the Ganga bank.
7. **Aurangzeb's mosque at Mathura** — on the Gobind Dev (Keshav Dev) Temple site.

Shourie's argument: the *English* translation (1977) censored these passages. The Urdu original by a senior Muslim scholar is the citation that's hard to dismiss.

### 2. The Ayodhya Muslim testimony (Vol I, Ch 9) — four pre-British Muslim sources

Harsh Narain cites Muslim sources *predating* British rule that describe the Babri Masjid as having displaced the Janmasthan temple:

1. **Application by Muhammad Ashghar (Khatib, Babri Masjid), 30 Nov 1858** — calls the mosque "masjid-i Janmasthan" and the courtyard "maqam Janmasthan ka."
2. **Mirza Jan, *Hadiqah-i Shuhada* (1856, Lucknow)** — eyewitness to the 1855 Jihad to recapture Hanuman Garhi; describes the Babri Masjid as built on the Janmasthan temple under patronage of Musa Ashiqan in 923 AH (1528 CE).
3. **Shykh Azamat Ali Kakorawi Nami, *Muraqqah-i Khusrawi / Tarikh-i Avadh* (completed 1869)** — "the Babari mosque was built up in 923 AH in the Janmasthan temple in Faizabad-Avadh, which was a great place of worship and capital of Rama's father."
4. **Mirza Rajab Ali Beg Surur, *Fasanah-i Ibrat* (Urdu novel, 19th c.)** — "a great mosque was built on the spot where Sita ki Rasoi is situated... The mosque was built in 923 AH under the patronage of Sayyid Mir Ashiqan."

### 4. Vol II Ch 6 — Epigraphic evidence (mosque inscriptions in situ)

Sample inscriptions cited (with archaeological survey provenance):

- **Jami Masjid, Srikakulam (AP), 1641–42 CE** — Arabic inscription: "the idol was broken and the mosque constructed by Sher Muhammad Khan Ghazi." (Discovered 1953–54.)
- **Mosque at Nuh (Gurgaon, Haryana), 1400 CE** — Persian inscription on entrance: foundation laid by Bahadur Khan Nahar in the reign of Muhammad Shah Tughlaq "from the materials of a Hindu temple in village Sainthali where Hindus used to assemble in large numbers every year."
- **Jami Masjid, Ritpur (Amraoti, Maharashtra), 1878 CE (rebuild)** — Persian inscription: originally built by Aurangzeb on the site of a Hindu temple, reconstructed 1295 AH.
- **Jami Masjid, Mahalla Sunhat, Balasore (Orissa), built ~1673 CE** — inscription: "in AH 1079 (1668–69) a mob of Muslim mendicants (faqirs) led by Talib stormed and set fire to the temple of Sri Chandi... Five years later the local faujdar built the mosque on the same site."
- **Jami Masjid, Tadpatri (AP), 1695 CE** — inscription records construction on the site of a temple by "Mahmud" in Aurangzeb's reign.
- **Patthar-ki-Masjid, Patna** — Dr. Bloch's record: inscription stating temple at Majhauli (Gorakhpur) demolished by Prince Parwiz (son of Jahangir) in 1626; materials shipped to Patna for mosque construction. (Now plastered over.)

### 5. Vol II Ch 7 — Literary evidence (Muslim chronicles, chronologically arranged)

Major sources cited (these are the citations Sadhaka should reach to when writing about specific events — not Goel's interpretation):

| Source | Author | Period covered |
|---|---|---|
| **Futuh-ul-Buldan** | al-Biladhuri (d. 893 CE) | Early Arab conquests inc. Seistan, Sindh; Muhammad bin Qasim at Debal, Multan |
| **Tarikh-i-Tabari** | al-Tabari (d. 922 CE) | Qutaibah bin Muslim's destructions in Khurasan |
| **Tarikh-i-Yamini** | Utbi | Mahmud of Ghazni's campaigns |
| **Kamil-fi-t-Tawarikh** | Ibn al-Athir | 13th c. comprehensive |
| **Tabaqat-i-Nasiri** | Minhaj-i-Siraj | Mu'izzuddin Ghori, Slave Sultans |
| **Tarikh-i-Firuz Shahi** | Ziauddin Barani | Khalji + Tughlaq period |
| **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri** | Jahangir himself | Mughal |
| **Maasir-i-Alamgiri** | Saqi Mustaid Khan | Aurangzeb — explicit temple demolition orders |
| **Futuhat-i-Alamgiri** | Ishwardas Nagar | Aurangzeb |

These are nearly all available in the H.M. Elliot & J. Dowson *History of India as Told by Its Own Historians* (8 vols, 1867–77), which the book draws on heavily. **Elliot & Dowson is the standard citable source** — it's a colonial-era compilation but the translations of the Muslim chroniclers themselves are the primary material.

## What is *not* useful for Sadhaka

- **Vol II Section IV** ("Islamic Theology of Iconoclasm" — Chs 8–16) — polemical, contains the Ka'ba-as-Shiva-temple speculation (Appendix 2) which is not credible scholarship. Skip.
- **The book's overall framing** that Islam-as-religion is the irreducible cause. This is a thesis, not an observation. There are competing scholarly framings (political-economic, dynastic, war-of-conquest patterns broadly shared across pre-modern empires). Sadhaka should report the *evidence of what was destroyed* and let readers situate the cause — not adopt the book's monocausal theology.
- **The Bangladesh 1989 list** (Vol I Appendix) — useful as historical record but very narrow scope; not relevant to Sadhaka's content surface.
- **The Marxist Professors questionnaire / Romila Thapar correspondence** — historiographic polemic; not relevant.

## How Sadhaka should use this

### KB claim files to seed (high priority, scoped)

Each should be a `backend/app/knowledge/kb/claims/*.md` file following the existing schema (plain-language statement → decomposed sub-claims with verdict tags → evidence for/against → scope boundaries). Tone neutral, scholarly, primary-source-first.

1. **`babri-masjid-temple-site-claim.md`** — "Was the Babri Masjid built on a pre-existing temple at Rama Janmasthan?" Decompose: (a) was there a temple at the site? — verdict supported by ASI 2003 report + Muslim-sourced testimony; (b) was Rama born there? — devotional claim, not historically adjudicable; (c) was it specifically a Janmasthan temple? — supported by Muslim sources from 1856–1869.

2. **`somnath-destruction-claim.md`** — "Did Mahmud of Ghazni destroy Somnath in 1026 CE?" Decompose: (a) destruction event — supported by multiple Muslim chronicles inc. al-Biruni, Utbi; (b) "seventeen times" framing — composite; (c) loot figures — likely exaggerated by court historians; (d) sociopolitical context.

3. **`kashi-vishwanath-aurangzeb-claim.md`** — "Did Aurangzeb destroy the Vishweshwar (Kashi Vishwanath) temple in 1669 CE?" Decompose: (a) the demolition order — supported by Aurangzeb's own firman in *Maasir-i-Alamgiri*; (b) the Gyanvapi mosque on the site — physical evidence + Maulana Abdul Hai's own admission; (c) the pre-existing temple structure — visible in mosque wall fabric.

4. **`keshavdev-mathura-aurangzeb-claim.md`** — "Did Aurangzeb destroy the Keshavdev (Krishna Janmasthan) temple in Mathura?" Decompose: (a) 1670 demolition firman — supported by *Maasir-i-Alamgiri*; (b) Shahi Eidgah on site — physical + Muslim-source confirmation.

5. **`nalanda-bakhtiyar-khilji-claim.md`** — "Did Bakhtiyar Khilji destroy Nalanda c. 1193?" Decompose: (a) destruction of a major Buddhist vihara at Bihar Sharif (Odantapuri) — supported by Tabaqat-i-Nasiri; (b) Nalanda proper — likely declined earlier and was burned in the same campaign; (c) "library burned for six months" claim — Tibetan source Taranatha, secondary.

6. **`martand-sun-temple-sikandar-butshikan-claim.md`** — "Did Sultan Sikandar Butshikan destroy the Martand Sun Temple and other Kashmir Hindu temples?" Decompose: (a) the title "Butshikan" (iconoclast) in Kashmir's own Persian chronicles — supported by *Tarikh-i-Rashidi*, *Tarikh-i-Hassan*; (b) specific destructions documented; (c) scale of conversion in Kashmir Valley.

7. **`rudramahalaya-sidhpur-claim.md`** — "Was the Sidhpur Jami Masjid built on the Rudramahalaya temple?" Decompose: (a) 1297 destruction by Ulugh Khan + 1415 by Ahmed Shah — supported by *Minorities' Commission 1983 Report* + ASI excavation that exposed Shiva linga; (b) physical conversion of cubicles into mosque — confirmed.

8. **`ayodhya-asi-2003-report-claim.md`** — "What did the ASI 2003 excavation under the Babri Masjid site find?" Decompose into specific finds (pillar bases, north-south wall, 50+ pillars, structural floor) — supported by court-record ASI report. This is the *evidentiary core* of the post-1990 case and is not in this book (the book predates it).

### Hub article opportunity

A scoped, scholarly article — **`/sacred-sites-destruction-medieval-india`** or similar — that:
- Opens with the scale: the book documents ~2000 monuments; archaeological survey records corroborate ~80% of entries that have been re-examined.
- Names the methodological constraint: most evidence is from Muslim chronicles and Islamic-period inscriptions (not Hindu sources, which are sparse for the period).
- Presents 5–7 well-documented case studies (Somnath, Kashi, Mathura, Martand, Rudramahalaya, Bidar, Vijayanagar-Hampi, Nalanda/Odantapuri).
- **Frames neutrally**: "what we know, what is contested, what is primary, what is later interpretation."
- **Cites the KB claim files** (the 8 above) rather than restating the polemical version.
- Links into the existing temple cluster (`/kailasa-temple-ellora`, `/south-india-temple-architecture`, `/most-powerful-shiva-temples-india`, `/kailasa-vs-ajanta-caves`).

### Companion architectural article opportunity

**`/temple-architecture-survives-medieval-destruction`** — why the surviving temple corpus skews south: the Vijayanagar buffer, the Cholas/Pandyas, the deeper Deccan, and the Eastern Ganga reach (Konark, Puri, Bhubaneswar). Treats Hampi specifically: the destruction of 1565 (post-Talikota) as a single, well-documented destruction event.

### What to deliberately *not* do

- **Don't write a polemic.** Sadhaka's value is in the spiritual/philosophical tradition; the destruction story is part of the historical record but the platform is not a Hindutva mouthpiece. Other platforms own that lane.
- **Don't cite Goel as the primary authority** — cite the Muslim chronicles and the ASI reports that Goel himself cites. The book is a research aggregator, not a primary source. The strength of the case lies in Muslim sources, not in Goel's framing.
- **Don't include Vol II Section IV theological material** (e.g., "Ka'ba was a Shiva temple"). It is not credible scholarship and would compromise Sadhaka's credibility.
- **Don't make claims about contemporary communalism.** The book's 1989–1993 framing is its own moment; do not transport it into 2026.

Its evidentiary apparatus is strong; its theological framing is polemical and should not be inherited.
