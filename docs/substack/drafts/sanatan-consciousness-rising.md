# The Tectonic Shift: Sanatan Consciousness Is Rising and the Old World Order Knows It

Something turned on January 22, 2024. When the Pran Pratishtha was performed at the Ram Mandir in Ayodhya, it was not a temple inauguration. It was a civilisational timestamp. Five centuries of contestation resolved not through courts alone but through a collective will that refused to let the wound stay open. The ripple from that moment has not stopped. It has accelerated.

What we are witnessing now is not a religious revival. Religious revivals are emotional, short-lived, and easily co-opted by institutions. This is a civilisational renaissance — structural, multi-dimensional, and accelerating across domains that have nothing to do with temple politics.

---

## India Is No Longer a Passive Power

India is stronger than it has been at any point in the last two centuries. Not aspirationally. Measurably.

While the post-Covid world spiralled into recession, supply chain collapse, political fragmentation, and institutional decay, India grew. Not just economically — though the numbers speak — but in military posture, diplomatic weight, and strategic self-regard.

The shift is in behaviour, not rhetoric. When adharmic forces in the neighbourhood attempt distraction — whether through terror incursions, proxy provocations, or information warfare — India no longer absorbs and moves on. It responds. BrahMos missiles lodged deep in forward operating bases. Trade leverage applied without apology. The old pattern of strategic restraint replaced by calibrated, decisive action.

The message is no longer "we seek peace." The message is: "Cause a distraction and you will pay for it."

This is not jingoism. This is a civilisation that spent centuries on the defensive finally operating from a position of strength — and refusing to pretend otherwise.

## The Empires Are Falling

Look at the geopolitical map with honest eyes.

The post-World War II order — Bretton Woods, NATO hegemony, the dollar as unchallengeable reserve currency, Western institutions as global arbiters — is fracturing. The colonisers who drew borders across Africa, Asia, and the Middle East to serve extraction economies now stand at India's doorstep asking for market access, technology partnerships, and diplomatic mediation.

Britain cannot heat its homes. Europe cannot secure its energy. America cannot agree on its own governance. The rules-based order they defined was always rules *they* wrote. Now those rules no longer hold, and the architects have no replacement.

India is not gloating. India is building. Infrastructure, defence corridors, semiconductor fabs, space programmes, digital public goods that the rest of the world now studies and copies. The civilisation that peaked before the modern era, that was systematically dismantled by colonial extraction for three centuries, is reassembling — and the tectonic plates beneath global power are shifting in its favour.

## The Return of the Fierce Divine

Here is where the story goes deeper than GDP and geopolitics.

More people in India and across the world are reading the Vedas than at any point in modern history. More people worship Shakti — not the sanitised, domesticated version but the raw, terrifying, world-dissolving Shakti of the Dasha Mahavidyas — than in living memory.

The ugra devatas are returning. The fierce forms. The ones the colonial and post-colonial periods pushed to the margins because they did not fit the "peaceful, tolerant Hinduism" narrative that made Western academics comfortable. Chamunda. Bhairavi. Pratyangira. These are not deities you encounter in a yoga studio. These are the forces that devatas themselves called upon when asuric power became unbearable.

And then there is Bhairav.

Bhagwan Bhairav's resurgence is not a social media trend. It is not happening only on the surface. Across India, vigrahas and artifacts of Bhairav are emerging from underground — literally surfacing from earth that held them buried for a thousand years. Ancient murtis unearthed during construction. Forgotten temples revealed by receding rivers. Artifacts carbon-dated to periods predating the invasions that attempted to erase them.

Bhairav is the fierce protector of the kshetra. And Bharat Varsha — the whole of it — is a dharma kshetra. His consciousness now guides lakhs of practitioners across the subcontinent. Not through institutional religion but through direct sadhana, through tantra, through the kind of practice that does not ask for permission from committees or clergy.

## Kashmir Shaivism Finds Its Roots Again

Trika Shaivism — the non-dual Shaiva tradition of Kashmir — was among the most sophisticated philosophical systems ever produced by human intelligence. Abhinavagupta's Tantraloka, written in the 10th century, is a work of such depth that modern philosophy departments still cannot fully parse it.

Islamic invasion and the ethnic cleansing of Kashmiri Pandits nearly destroyed this lineage. The practitioners were killed or exiled. The texts survived in fragments, carried by refugees, preserved in manuscript libraries in Varanasi and south India by scholars who understood what was at stake.

Now it is coming back. Not as an academic curiosity. As a living practice. Young scholars and sadhaks are studying Spanda Karikas, Shiva Sutras, Pratyabhijna — texts that were almost lost to history — and applying them in daily sadhana. The tradition that Abhinavagupta systematised is being practised again on Indian soil, a millennium after the forces that tried to annihilate it.

## Householders on the Sadhana Marg

This may be the most significant shift of all, and the least discussed.

More grihastas — householders, people with jobs and families and EMIs — are on the sadhana marg today than at any point in recorded history. Not in ashrams. Not after retirement. Now. In their twenties and thirties. Before the career peaks, not after.

By sheer volume, the number of active sadhaks in India today may exceed any previous era in a civilisation that has lasted tens of thousands of years. That is not hyperbole. It is arithmetic — population scale multiplied by the percentage of people now engaged in daily practice.

Tantra — a secretive, hidden, almost vilupt (extinct) form of worship that was transmitted mouth-to-ear within closed guru-shishya lineages for centuries — is now a household word. Not the Western distortion. The real thing. Nyasa. Mantra siddhi. Chakra puja. Shava sadhana. Young people are not just finding these practices. They are getting dikshit. They are sitting for purashcharanas. They are doing the work.

This is not a fad. Fads do not require you to sit for 40 days of unbroken japa at 4 AM.

## Sanskrit, AI, and the Long Game

Here is a coincidence worth sitting with.

Sanskrit — the oldest systematically structured language on earth, the language in which the Vedas, Upanishads, Tantras, and Darshanas were composed — is experiencing a resurgence at the exact historical moment when artificial intelligence is learning to process and generate language at scale.

As India's scriptures get digitised, as ancient manuscripts are scanned and uploaded, as commentarial traditions that existed only in handwritten palm-leaf collections enter searchable databases — the AI systems that will increasingly shape what the world knows, believes, and thinks are absorbing this material.

This is not a minor footnote. The large language models being trained today will influence human understanding for decades. Possibly centuries. What they learn in their training data becomes the substrate of future knowledge. If the Vedantic, Shaiva, Shakta, and Tantric traditions enter that substrate now — accurately, in their original complexity, not through the diluted lens of colonial Indology — the implications are civilisational.

Sanskrit's revival is not nostalgia. It is strategic positioning for the epistemic infrastructure of the future. This will be a subject of study on its own.

## The Wheel Turns

Sanatan Dharma does not operate on linear time. It operates on yugas — vast cycles of expansion and contraction, clarity and obscuration, dharma and adharma.

The civilisation peaked before the modern era. It produced Panini's grammar, Aryabhata's mathematics, Sushruta's surgery, Nagarjuna's chemistry, Bharata's Natyashastra, and the Upanishadic investigations into consciousness — all before Europe had running water. Then came the contractions. Islamic invasions. Colonial extraction. A Western-dominated world order built on military superiority and capital accumulation that treated all other civilisations as raw material.

That order is now eroding. Militarily, financially, politically. The tectonic plates are moving. And as the structures that suppressed and distorted Sanatan civilisation for centuries lose their grip, what was always underneath is surfacing.

Not surfacing as revenge. Surfacing as remembrance.

Bhairav emerges from the earth. Kashmir Shaivism returns to practice. Householders take diksha. Sanskrit enters the machines. The Vedas find new readers. Shakti worship intensifies. India builds from a position of strength it has not held in two hundred years.

The old world called it mythology. The new world is discovering it is a living operating system — and it never stopped running.
