## 1. Strategic frame

### 1.1 What Post 0 must accomplish
A manifesto is a founding statement, not a single-thesis argument essay. Its job is to plant the worldview the whole publication hangs off and to make the reader want the next four issues. Concretely, it must do five things:

1. **Name the lens** — Sanatana dharma read as an operating system for consciousness, not a religion to believe: an account of what a mind is, how reality is structured, and how to act inside it.
2. **State the inversion** — modern science treats consciousness as an output to be explained or engineered; the tradition treats it as the ground, the witness (sākṣī) that every explanation already presupposes. That inversion is the publication's central, ownable claim.
3. **Declare the method** — one idea per issue, traced from its genesis in the primary texts to what it implies for a life now; rigorous enough to trust, concrete enough to argue with.
4. **Say who it is for and what they get** — intellectually curious readers who already lean this way and want fodder and discussion points, not lifestyle advice. The payoff is a transferable worldview, not a tip.
5. **Foreshadow the warmup arc** — seed the four essays to come (the observer/sākṣī vs. the machine, the witness and death, niṣkāma karma as decision theory, the yuga lens) so the manifesto reads as the doorway to a body of work.

### 1.2 Target reader
Not a beginner and not a devotee. A reader who has already encountered these ideas (read a Gita, knows what Advaita is, has opinions) and wants intellectual companionship at a high register: someone who reads Aeon, the LRB, Ribbonfarm, or follows the consciousness/AI discourse and senses that the tradition has something load-bearing to say about it. The essay treats this reader as a fellow intellectual, never a student. Zero hand-holding, zero basics.

### 1.3 The one core claim (LOCKED)
> Sanatana dharma is not a religion to believe but an operating system for consciousness: an account of what a mind is, how reality is structured, and how to act inside it. In an age racing to manufacture minds, it is the most complete map of the one thing the machines do not have.

This is a manifesto's *founding* claim, so it is broader than a normal single-thesis essay. To keep it arguable (the `/write-newsletter` rigor gate requires a genuine counter-position), it carries one sharp, defensible sub-claim that does the load-bearing work:

> **Sub-claim (the spine):** Modern science treats consciousness as an output to be explained or engineered (the hard problem; the AI-sentience project). Sanatana philosophy treats it as the *ground*: the witness (sākṣī) that all explanation already presupposes. That inversion yields a more coherent account of mind than the computational one now being built into machines.

The spine must stay genuinely arguable. The honest counter is that mechanism (computational neuroscience, AI) is closing in on consciousness and the tradition is romantic nostalgia; the essay must hold that open in Beat 5 before answering it.

### 1.4 Three-to-five takeaways ("fodder" the reader carries away)
The reader should leave able to *say these at a dinner table* and defend them:

1. **"Sanatana dharma is an OS, not a faith."** It specifies what a mind is, what reality is, and how an agent operates inside it. Belief is optional; the structure is the claim. (Modern bridge: an operating system runs regardless of whether the user believes in it.)
2. **"Modern science asks how consciousness is produced; the tradition asks what already has to be there for the question to be askable."** One treats awareness as an output; the other treats it as the ground.
3. **"The tradition's deepest move is the witness."** Across Upanishad, Gita, and Advaita, the recurring instruction is to locate the sākṣī, the awareness to which experience appears. This is the thread that connects consciousness studies, the AI race, decision-making, and death.
4. **"The how-to-live is an implication, not an instruction."** The publication never tells the reader what to do. It traces an idea precisely enough that the practical consequence becomes obvious and the reader draws it.
5. **"The machines are being built to do everything except be the one thing the tradition spent millennia mapping."** (Bridges forward to the observer-and-the-machine essay.)

### 1.5 Tone calibration
Essayist intellectual register, third person throughout (no "we"/"you"/"I"/"one" as pronoun). Calibrate against the benchmark article's prose: declarative, citation-woven, steel-mans its opposition, never reverent. Provocative without being contrarian. A manifesto can carry slightly more *declarative cadence* and *programmatic structure* than a standard essay, but it must stay cited and must not drift into devotional or motivational register. The closing lands as implication, not exhortation.

- If the witness was only ever watching, the fear of its own ending is a category error the tradition already diagnosed. (Seed of the witness-and-death essay — Post 2.)
